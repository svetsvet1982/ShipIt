## 대본

### 도입 — 진료실, Lily의 경과 진료

ELENA: Okay, Lily. Let's take a look. Chin up a little.

LILY: Be honest, Doctor. How bad is it?

ELENA: Honestly? Better than I expected. The skin survived.

LILY: But there's a mark. I can see it in the mirror.

ELENA: A small pink mark on the tip. It will fade.

NOAH: Completely?

ELENA: Mostly. In six months, we can try a laser if needed.

LILY: Six months. Okay. I can live with that.

ELENA: You were lucky, Lily. Some patients aren't. You came in fast, and the enzyme worked in time, but I've seen cases where an hour's delay meant permanent damage.

LILY: Thank you. Really. You saved my face.

ELENA: You're welcome. And no more Instagram deals, please.

LILY: Never again. I promise.

NOAH: Dr. Ruiz, do you have ten minutes after this?

ELENA: I have three more patients. Then maybe.

LILY: Say yes, please. He hasn't stopped talking about it for ten days.

### 장면1 — 원장실, 오후 5시: 제안

ELENA: All right, Noah. Ten minutes. The clock is running.

NOAH: Thank you. Remember the fishing net? The knots that break?

ELENA: I remember. I thought about it on my drive home.

NOAH: I found my chemist. She tested it in grad school.

ELENA: And?

NOAH: In a test tube, it dissolved two point four times faster.

ELENA: A test tube isn't a patient. But it's interesting.

NOAH: I also found someone for manufacturing. Ex-Army. Very serious.

ELENA: You've been busy. Did you sleep at all?

NOAH: A little. And I quit my job at Halvern.

ELENA: You quit? Because of one night in my ER?

NOAH: Because of what I saw that night. Yes.

ELENA: That's either very brave or very foolish.

NOAH: Probably both. Which is why I'm here.

ELENA: Go on.

NOAH: We need a doctor on the team. Not an advisor who reads a report once a month, but a co-founder who helps make the decisions, especially the hard ones about safety.

ELENA: You want me to join your company?

NOAH: As Chief Medical Officer. You'd lead the clinical side.

ELENA: Noah, I have a clinic. Patients. Staff who depend on me. I built this practice over many years, and I can't just walk away from it for a napkin drawing.

NOAH: I know. I'm not asking you to close it.

ELENA: And I still take ER calls twice a month.

NOAH: That's exactly where you saw the problem.

ELENA: Don't use my own story against me. That's a marketing trick.

NOAH: Fair. Sorry. Old habits.

ELENA: Why me? There are hundreds of dermatologists in Texas.

NOAH: Because you said it was a contradiction. But you didn't say no.

ELENA: Ha. You remember that.

NOAH: And you said complications show up in your ER, not on sales reports.

ELENA: That's true. How many do you think I see a year?

NOAH: I don't know. I promised my chemist data. I have none.

ELENA: Eleven this year. Just me. Just in Austin.

NOAH: Eleven. And how many get reported?

ELENA: Officially? Maybe two. Most cases are never reported, because the paperwork takes time, and many doctors simply treat the patient and move on to the next emergency.

NOAH: So the real number is much higher than anyone thinks.

ELENA: Nobody knows the real number. That's the scary part.

### 장면2 — 우려 다루기

NOAH: Dr. Ruiz, what's stopping you? Tell me your concerns.

ELENA: How long do you have?

NOAH: As long as you need. Forget the ten minutes.

ELENA: First, my patients. They come first. Always.

NOAH: I hear your concerns. Your clinic stays open, and you keep your patients. We'd only need your time in the evenings and some weekends, at least until we raise real money.

ELENA: Second, conflict of interest. If I own part of a filler company…

NOAH: Then your patients might wonder why you recommend it.

ELENA: Exactly. Doctors with equity must disclose it, and patients notice. If a patient ever thinks I'm recommending something for my own profit, I've lost their trust forever.

NOAH: So we disclose everything. Fully. From day one.

ELENA: And I never push our product on my own patients.

NOAH: Agreed. Not on your patients.

ELENA: Not on my patients. Good. Third, my reputation.

NOAH: What about it?

ELENA: I've seen good doctors become salespeople. They start with one company, then they're on stage at every conference, smiling for the camera. I won't become one of them.

NOAH: We don't need a salesperson. I'm the salesperson.

ELENA: Then what do you need me for, exactly?

NOAH: To tell us the truth, especially when we don't want to hear it. I'll be excited, investors will be impatient, and somebody in the room has to say no.

ELENA: That, I can do. I do it every day.

NOAH: And to design the clinical trial. And to talk to the FDA.

ELENA: Fourth concern. Time. I have a fourteen-year-old son.

NOAH: What's his name?

ELENA: Mateo. He plays soccer on Saturdays. I don't miss games.

NOAH: Then we never schedule anything on Saturday mornings.

ELENA: You're making a lot of promises, Noah.

NOAH: Then hold me to them. Get it all in writing.

ELENA: I will. Last question. Why should I trust a marketing guy?

NOAH: Because I wrote "Results that stay."

ELENA: That's not a reason to trust you. That's a reason not to.

NOAH: It means I know exactly what marketing hides, because I helped hide it. I know which risks go in small print, so I'll know where to look for them.

ELENA: Hmm. That's an honest answer. I'll give you that.

### 절정·전환 — Priya 등장

PRIYA: Sorry I'm late. Traffic on Mopac was a nightmare.

NOAH: Dr. Ruiz, this is Priya Raman. The chemist.

ELENA: The famous chapter four. Nice to meet you, Priya.

PRIYA: Nice to meet you. Noah talks about you like a superhero.

ELENA: He talks about everything like that. Can I ask you a hard question?

PRIYA: Please. I prefer hard questions.

ELENA: Fillers are sterilized with heat. Will your weak links survive the autoclave?

PRIYA: Good question. Marcus asked Noah the same thing last week.

ELENA: And the answer?

PRIYA: The honest answer is, I don't know yet.

ELENA: Good. "I don't know" is the most trustworthy answer in science.

PRIYA: I can tell you how I'd find out. Three tests, about six weeks. Each test checks something different, and if the gel survives all three, then we'll have real data instead of hope.

ELENA: And if it fails?

PRIYA: Then we redesign the link, or we stop. I won't fall in love with my own chemistry. Data decides, not Noah, and definitely not the investors.

NOAH: Hey. I'm standing right here.

ELENA: I like her already, Noah.

PRIYA: Dr. Ruiz, can I ask you a question too?

ELENA: Call me Elena. Go ahead.

PRIYA: If this works, would you actually use it? On your own patients?

ELENA: If the data is good, yes. Without hesitation.

PRIYA: Then that's the only endorsement I care about.

NOAH: So, Elena. What would it take?

ELENA: What would it take. Let me think.

PRIYA: Take your time. He'll wait. Impatiently.

ELENA: One condition above everything else.

### 마무리 — 조건과 합류

NOAH: Name it.

ELENA: If I say a product is not safe, it doesn't ship. Period.

NOAH: Even if investors are screaming? Even if we're out of money?

ELENA: Especially then. That's when companies cut corners. When the money is running out and everyone is tired, the pressure to ship becomes enormous, and that's exactly when patients get hurt.

PRIYA: I agree with that completely.

NOAH: Then it's a deal. You have the final say on safety.

ELENA: In writing.

NOAH: In writing. With a lawyer. We're finding one this week.

ELENA: Then I'm in. Part-time at first, until there's real money.

NOAH: Thank you, Elena. Really.

ELENA: Don't thank me yet. First, do no harm. That's my rule.

PRIYA: First, do no harm. I like that as a company rule too.

ELENA: Now, the next step. Who owns what in this company?

NOAH: We haven't talked about that yet.

ELENA: Then talk about it soon. Saturday afternoon, after Mateo's game. Bring everyone.

### ② 비즈니스 컬처 노트 — 의사와 이해충돌 공개
미국에서는 의사가 제약·의료기기 회사로부터 받는 돈과 지분이 **Open Payments(Sunshine Act)** 제도를 통해 공개된다. 그래서 의사가 회사에 참여할 때는 환자와 학회에 관계를 공개(disclose)하고, 자신의 환자에게 해당 제품을 권할 때 특히 조심해야 한다. "Not on my patients."라는 Elena의 말은 이런 윤리 기준을 반영한다. 또 "First, do no harm(무엇보다 해를 끼치지 말라)"은 히포크라테스 전통에서 온 의료 윤리의 대표 문구다.

### ③ 실무 템플릿 — 우려를 정리하고 답하는 순서 (Objection Handling)
1. **Ask** — What's stopping you? / Tell me your concerns.
2. **Acknowledge** — I hear your concerns. / That's fair.
3. **Answer** — Here's what we can do: **[solution]**.
4. **Confirm** — Does that address your concern?

예시:
> *A: My second concern is conflict of interest.*
> *B: That's fair. Here's what we can do: we disclose everything, from day one, and you never recommend the product to your own patients. Does that address your concern?*

### ④ 긴 발화 분석

> **ELENA (마무리):** "Especially then. That's when companies cut corners. When the money is running out and everyone is tired, the pressure to ship becomes enormous, and that's exactly when patients get hurt."

| 구조 | 영어 | 기능 |
|---|---|---|
| ① 반전 강조 | Especially then. | 상대의 예외 질문을 오히려 핵심 근거로 뒤집음 |
| ② 일반 원칙 | That's when companies cut corners. | "That's when…"으로 경험에서 나온 원칙을 제시 |
| ③ 상황 묘사(When절) | When the money is running out and everyone is tired, the pressure to ship becomes enormous, | 압박이 커지는 구체적 장면을 그려 줌 |
| ④ 결과(최악) | and that's exactly when patients get hurt. | 같은 "that's when"을 반복해 결론을 못 박음 |

**활용 패턴:** "Especially then." → "That's when A." → "When B, C." → "that's exactly when D."
**연결 표현:** especially then(그럴 때일수록) · cut corners(편법을 쓰다, 절차를 생략하다) · that's exactly when…(바로 그때 ~한다)
**연습:** "마감이 급해도 검수를 생략할 수 없다"는 원칙을 같은 구조로 30단어 안팎으로 설명해 보기.

### 장면2 — 우려 다루기

엘레나: 둘째, 이해충돌이요. 내가 필러 회사 지분을 가지면…

노아: 환자들이 왜 그 제품을 권하는지 의심할 수 있겠죠.

엘레나: 바로 그거예요. 지분이 있는 의사는 반드시 공개해야 하고, 환자들도 알아채요. 내가 내 이익 때문에 뭔가를 권한다고 환자가 한 번이라도 생각하면, 그 신뢰는 영영 잃는 거예요.

노아: 그럼 전부 공개해요. 완전히. 첫날부터요.

엘레나: 그리고 내 환자들한테 우리 제품을 절대 강요하지 않을 거예요.

노아: 동의해요. 선생님 환자들한텐 안 하는 걸로.

엘레나: 내 환자들한텐 안 된다. 좋아요. 셋째, 내 평판이요.

노아: 평판이 어때서요?

엘레나: 좋은 의사가 영업사원이 되는 걸 봤어요. 회사 하나로 시작해서, 나중엔 모든 학회 무대에 올라 카메라 앞에서 웃고 있죠. 난 그런 사람이 되지 않을 거예요.

노아: 영업사원은 필요 없어요. 영업은 제가 해요.

엘레나: 그럼 정확히 나는 뭐가 필요한 거죠?

노아: 진실을 말해 주시는 거요. 특히 우리가 듣기 싫어할 때요. 저는 들떠 있을 거고, 투자자들은 조급해할 거예요. 그 자리에서 누군가는 '안 된다'고 말해야 해요.

엘레나: 그건 할 수 있어요. 매일 하는 일이니까.

노아: 그리고 임상시험을 설계하고, FDA와 얘기하는 일도요.

엘레나: 넷째 걱정. 시간이요. 열네 살 아들이 있어요.

노아: 아드님 이름이 뭐예요?

엘레나: 마테오요. 토요일마다 축구를 해요. 난 경기를 빠지지 않아요.

노아: 그럼 토요일 오전엔 절대 일정을 안 잡을게요.

엘레나: 약속을 너무 많이 하네요, 노아.

노아: 그럼 지키게 만드세요. 전부 서면으로 받아 두세요.

엘레나: 그럴 거예요. 마지막 질문. 마케팅 하는 사람을 왜 믿어야 하죠?

노아: 제가 "머무는 결과"를 썼으니까요.

엘레나: 그건 믿을 이유가 아니에요. 믿지 말아야 할 이유죠.

노아: 마케팅이 뭘 숨기는지 정확히 안다는 뜻이에요. 저도 숨기는 데 한몫했으니까요. 어떤 리스크가 작은 글씨로 들어가는지 알기 때문에, 어디를 봐야 하는지도 알 거예요.

엘레나: 흠. 솔직한 대답이네요. 그건 인정할게요.

### 절정·전환 — Priya 등장

프리야: 늦어서 죄송해요. 모팩 고속도로가 완전 지옥이었어요.

노아: 루이스 선생님, 이쪽은 프리야 라만이에요. 그 화학자요.

엘레나: 그 유명한 4장의 주인공이군요. 반가워요, 프리야.

프리야: 반갑습니다. 노아가 선생님을 슈퍼히어로처럼 얘기하던데요.

엘레나: 이 사람은 뭐든 그렇게 얘기하죠. 어려운 질문 하나 해도 될까요?

프리야: 해 주세요. 전 어려운 질문이 더 좋아요.

엘레나: 필러는 열로 멸균하잖아요. 그 약한 연결고리가 고압 멸균을 견딜까요?

프리야: 좋은 질문이에요. 마커스도 지난주에 노아한테 똑같은 걸 물었대요.

엘레나: 그래서 답은요?

프리야: 솔직한 답은, 아직 모른다는 거예요.

엘레나: 좋아요. "모르겠다"는 과학에서 가장 믿을 만한 대답이에요.

프리야: 어떻게 알아낼지는 말씀드릴 수 있어요. 시험 세 가지, 6주쯤이요. 시험마다 확인하는 게 다르고, 젤이 세 가지를 다 견디면 그때는 희망 대신 진짜 데이터가 생기는 거예요.

엘레나: 실패하면요?

프리야: 그럼 연결고리를 다시 설계하거나, 멈추는 거죠. 제 화학에 빠져서 눈이 멀진 않을 거예요. 노아가 아니라 데이터가 결정해요. 투자자는 더더욱 아니고요.

노아: 저기요. 나 바로 여기 서 있거든.

엘레나: 벌써 이분이 마음에 드네요, 노아.

프리야: 루이스 선생님, 저도 하나 여쭤봐도 될까요?

엘레나: 엘레나라고 불러요. 물어보세요.

프리야: 이게 성공하면, 정말 쓰실 거예요? 선생님 환자들한테요?

엘레나: 데이터가 좋다면요. 망설임 없이.

프리야: 그럼 그게 제가 신경 쓰는 유일한 추천이에요.

노아: 그래서 엘레나. 어떻게 하면 함께하실 수 있을까요?

엘레나: 어떻게 하면이라. 생각 좀 해 볼게요.

프리야: 천천히 생각하세요. 얘는 기다릴 거예요. 안절부절못하면서.

엘레나: 무엇보다 중요한 조건이 하나 있어요.

### 마무리 — 조건과 합류

노아: 말씀하세요.
