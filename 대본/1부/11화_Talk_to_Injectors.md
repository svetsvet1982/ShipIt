## 대본

### 도입 — Elena의 차 안, 오전 9시

ELENA: Before we go in, some rules. Customer interviews are not sales calls.

NOAH: Got it. No selling.

ELENA: No selling. No pitching. And don't describe our idea at all.

NOAH: Not at all? Then what do we talk about?

ELENA: Their problems. Their past. What they actually did, not what they'd do. Ask them to tell you about a real day, a real patient, and let them walk you through it.

NOAH: Why not ask what they'd do?

ELENA: Because people are polite. They'll say, "Great idea!" And never buy it.

NOAH: So past behavior is more honest than future promises.

ELENA: Exactly. And listen more than you talk. Eighty-twenty.

NOAH: Eighty percent listening. That's hard for a marketing guy.

ELENA: I know. That's why I'm driving. So I can watch you.

NOAH: How many interviews today?

ELENA: Three. Plus the nine we did last week. Twelve total.

NOAH: And none of them are Halvern's big accounts. Diane would kill me.

ELENA: None. Research only, not selling. We're here. First stop.

### 장면1 — 메디스파, 간호사 시술자 Jasmine

JASMINE: Hi! Dr. Ruiz? I'm Jasmine. Thanks for coming to us.

ELENA: Thank you for your time, Jasmine. This is Noah, my colleague.

NOAH: Nice to meet you. We'll keep it to thirty minutes.

JASMINE: Sure. What do you want to know?

NOAH: How long have you been injecting fillers?

JASMINE: Six years. I'm a nurse. I do about fifteen patients a day.

ELENA: That's busy. Walk me through your last complication.

JASMINE: Hmm. The last serious one? About four months ago.

NOAH: What happened?

JASMINE: I was injecting a nasolabial fold. Routine, something I do every day. Then the skin went white. Instantly. I knew right away what it meant, because they warn you about it in training.

ELENA: Blanching. What did you do next?

JASMINE: Honestly? For about ten seconds, I froze.

NOAH: That must have felt like a long ten seconds.

JASMINE: The longest of my life. Then I grabbed the hyaluronidase from the fridge.

NOAH: Wouldn't you love a filler that dissolves much faster?

ELENA: Noah. Let's hold that thought. Jasmine, how long did it take to resolve?

NOAH: Sorry. Go on, please.

JASMINE: About ninety minutes. Three rounds of enzyme, and after each one I waited and watched the skin. I called our medical director twice, because I wasn't sure it was working.

ELENA: Was the medical director on site?

JASMINE: No. He supervises four spas. He was across town.

NOAH: How did the patient do?

JASMINE: She was okay. Small bruise. But I didn't sleep that night.

ELENA: What did you do differently after that?

JASMINE: I keep two vials of enzyme ready now. And I took a weekend course.

NOAH: Who paid for the course?

JASMINE: Me. Six hundred dollars. The spa wouldn't pay.

ELENA: Did you report the complication anywhere?

JASMINE: To my medical director. Officially? I don't think he reported it.

NOAH: Can I ask what filler you were using?

JASMINE: Lumaderm. It's what most spas around here use.

NOAH: Of course it is.

ELENA: Last question. What worries you most when you inject?

JASMINE: That one day it won't be a bruise. That it'll be someone's eye. Every time I inject near the nose, I think about it, and I know the enzyme might not work fast enough.

ELENA: Thank you, Jasmine. That was very honest.

JASMINE: Thanks for asking. Nobody ever asks us nurses.

### 장면2 — 성형외과 Dr. Pierce, 메디스파 원장 Tiffany

ELENA: In the car, Noah. What did you do wrong?

NOAH: I asked a leading question. "Wouldn't you love…?"

ELENA: Right. A leading question gets a polite yes. A useless yes. You told her the answer you wanted, so she won't tell you what she really thinks. Ask about what happened instead.

NOAH: Noted. It won't happen again.

PIERCE: Elena! Long time. And you brought a friend.

ELENA: Alan, this is Noah. Thanks for squeezing us in.

PIERCE: For you, anytime. So, complications? I'll make it easy. I've never had one.

NOAH: Never? In how many years?

PIERCE: Twenty years. Good technique. Good anatomy. No problems.

ELENA: Everyone says that until the day they don't.

PIERCE: Ha. Always the pessimist, Elena.

NOAH: What matters most to you when you choose a filler?

PIERCE: How long it lasts. My patients pay a lot, and they don't want to come back so soon. They want results that stay. Everything else is secondary.

NOAH: Results that stay. I've heard that somewhere.

PIERCE: It's Halvern's slogan. Brilliant line. Whoever wrote it deserves a raise.

TIFFANY: Welcome to Glow Bar! I'm Tiffany. I own this location and two others.

NOAH: Thanks for having us. Can you tell us how you choose your fillers?

TIFFANY: Price, mostly. Patients compare prices on Instagram.

ELENA: Tell me about the last time something went wrong here.

TIFFANY: Last year. A vascular occlusion. The patient threatened to sue.

NOAH: What happened after that?

TIFFANY: We settled. It was cheaper than going to court, and I didn't want the publicity. Then my insurance went up forty percent, and it hasn't come back down.

ELENA: Forty percent. That's a lot.

TIFFANY: It's killing my margins. Insurance is my second biggest cost now.

NOAH: What would change that for you?

TIFFANY: Fewer complications. Or faster fixes. Lawsuits come from bad outcomes, not from small bruises. If a problem is fixed in minutes instead of hours, the patient goes home happy and doesn't call a lawyer.

NOAH: Would you pay more for a product that lowered that risk?

ELENA: Noah.

NOAH: Sorry. Let me rephrase. How did you decide on your current insurance?

TIFFANY: My broker showed me three options. I picked the one I could afford.

### 절정·전환 — 디브리핑, 오후 6시

PRIYA: So? How did it go? Show me the data.

NOAH: Twelve interviews total. Nurses, doctors, and spa owners.

PRIYA: How many had a vascular occlusion?

NOAH: Nine out of twelve. At least once.

PRIYA: Nine out of twelve. That's seventy-five percent.

ELENA: Small sample. But a strong signal.

PRIYA: How many keep hyaluronidase ready?

NOAH: All twelve. Every single one.

PRIYA: And how many said the enzyme works fast enough?

ELENA: Zero. Not one.

PRIYA: Okay. Now that's data.

NOAH: There's one surprise, though. Something we didn't expect.

PRIYA: What?

NOAH: Insurance. Spa owners worry about lawsuits more than anything. One bad case can raise their premium, and that hits their margins every single month, whether they have another complication or not.

ELENA: Tiffany's premium went up forty percent after one case.

PRIYA: So for them, faster dissolving isn't just safety. It's money.

NOAH: Exactly. Doctors buy safety. Owners buy lower risk.

ELENA: And nurses like Jasmine buy peace of mind. She froze for ten seconds.

PRIYA: Three customers. Three different reasons to care.

NOAH: That's the most useful thing we learned. Same product, different value. If we pitch everyone the same way, we'll miss most of them, so each customer needs their own message.

ELENA: Also, one doctor was skeptical. Alan Pierce. Twenty years, "no complications."

PRIYA: Is that possible?

ELENA: Possible. Or he doesn't remember. Or he doesn't report. When something goes wrong and resolves quickly, a lot of doctors don't count it as a complication. Jasmine's director probably didn't report hers either.

NOAH: He quoted my slogan. "Results that stay." He said whoever wrote it deserves a raise.

PRIYA: Did you tell him it was you?

### 마무리 — 다음 단계

NOAH: No. Rule one of customer interviews: don't talk about yourself.

ELENA: He learns fast. After two mistakes.

NOAH: Two leading questions. I'm keeping count, don't worry.

PRIYA: So what's the conclusion?

ELENA: The problem is real. It's common. And it's underreported.

NOAH: And people are already spending money on it. Courses, enzyme, insurance. Jasmine paid six hundred dollars of her own money for a weekend course. That tells me the pain is real.

PRIYA: That's a problem worth solving.

NOAH: Now we need a story worth funding.

ELENA: You mean a pitch deck.

NOAH: Ten slides. Problem, solution, market, team, the ask.

PRIYA: And please, Noah. Don't make it look like a napkin.

NOAH: Then I'll need a designer.

ELENA: Do you know one?

NOAH: I know one who works for tacos. And who already drew our logo.

PRIYA: Lily. Of course. Just don't let her put her nose in the deck.

### ② 비즈니스 컬처 노트 — "The Mom Test"
스타트업 업계에서 널리 읽히는 고객 인터뷰 원칙이다. "엄마한테 아이디어를 물어보면 무조건 좋다고 한다"는 데서 이름이 왔다. 핵심은 세 가지다. ① 내 아이디어가 아니라 **상대의 삶과 문제**에 대해 묻는다. ② 미래의 가정(Would you…?)이 아니라 **과거의 구체적 행동**(When was the last time…?)을 묻는다. ③ 말하기보다 듣는다. 이번 화에서 Elena가 Noah에게 가르치는 규칙이 바로 이것이다.

### ③ 실무 템플릿 — 인터뷰 인사이트 요약 (Interview Debrief)

> **Interviews:** **[number]** (**[types of people]**)
> **Key numbers:** **[X out of Y]** experienced **[problem]**.
> **Biggest surprise:** **[unexpected insight]**.
> **Different customers, different reasons:**
> – **[Customer A]** cares about **[value]**.
> – **[Customer B]** cares about **[value]**.
> **Conclusion:** The problem is **[real / common / costly]**.

예시:
> *Interviews: 12 (nurses, doctors, spa owners). Key numbers: 9 out of 12 experienced a vascular occlusion. Biggest surprise: spa owners worry most about insurance costs. Doctors care about safety, owners care about risk, nurses care about peace of mind. Conclusion: the problem is real, common, and underreported.*

### ④ 긴 발화 분석

> **ELENA (장면2):** "Right. A leading question gets a polite yes. A useless yes. You told her the answer you wanted, so she won't tell you what she really thinks. Ask about what happened instead."

| 구조 | 영어 | 기능 |
|---|---|---|
| ① 원리(짧은 규칙) | Right. A leading question gets a polite yes. A useless yes. | 상대의 실수를 인정한 뒤, 왜 문제인지 한 줄 규칙으로 정리 |
| ② 진단(무슨 일이 있었나) | You told her the answer you wanted, | 실제 상황에서 무엇이 잘못됐는지 구체적으로 짚음 |
| ③ 결과(so) | so she won't tell you what she really thinks. | "so"로 그 행동이 낳는 결과를 연결 |
| ④ 대안(instead) | Ask about what happened instead. | 비판으로 끝내지 않고 바로 쓸 수 있는 대안 제시 |

**활용 패턴:** 규칙 → 구체적 진단 → so 결과 → instead 대안
**연결 표현:** A useless yes(쓸모없는 "네") · so(그래서) · …instead(대신 ~하라)
**연습:** 회의에서 실수한 후배에게 같은 구조로 30단어 안팎의 건설적인 피드백을 해 보기.
