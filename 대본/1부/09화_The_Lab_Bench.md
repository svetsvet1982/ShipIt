## 대본

### 도입 — Capital BioWorks 로비, 오전 8시

RITA: Welcome to Capital BioWorks. I'm Rita Gomez, the lab manager.

NOAH: Noah Bennett. And this is Marcus Webb. Thanks for the tour.

RITA: My pleasure. First startup?

NOAH: First startup. First day, actually. My first full-time day.

RITA: Then welcome to the club. Coffee's free. Everything else costs money.

MARCUS: That's the most honest sentence I've heard this month.

RITA: I've been doing this ten years. Honesty saves time.

NOAH: How many companies work here?

RITA: Twenty-two. Mostly biotech, plus two medical device teams. And one makes edible packaging, which surprises everyone. We take anyone who needs a bench and follows the safety rules.

MARCUS: Edible packaging. Interesting neighbors.

RITA: They bring snacks on Fridays. You'll like them.

NOAH: So, what do we get for a startup our size?

RITA: A bench. One lab bench, a shelf, and shared equipment.

NOAH: One bench. That's our whole company.

RITA: Every company here started with one bench. Follow me.

### 장면1 — 실험실 투어와 임대 조건

RITA: This is the wet lab. Your bench would be number fourteen.

MARCUS: Near the fume hood. Good. What about the autoclave?

RITA: Down the hall. Shared. You book it on our online calendar.

MARCUS: How far ahead do people usually book?

RITA: About a week. Two weeks before holidays. Everyone panics in December.

NOAH: What does a bench cost?

RITA: Twelve hundred a month. That includes shared equipment and basic supplies, so you don't have to buy a centrifuge or an autoclave on day one. For a new startup, that matters.

NOAH: Is there a minimum contract?

RITA: Six months is standard.

NOAH: Six months is a long time for us. To be honest, we haven't raised money yet, and I don't want to sign for more than we can pay. Is there any flexibility?

RITA: What did you have in mind?

NOAH: Month-to-month. We're raising money right now.

RITA: Month-to-month is fifteen hundred. The discount is for commitment.

MARCUS: How about three months, at twelve hundred?

RITA: Three months at thirteen hundred. And I'll waive the setup fee.

NOAH: What's the setup fee?

RITA: Five hundred. Safety training, badges, and a locker.

MARCUS: Three months at twelve fifty, setup fee waived. Final offer.

RITA: Ha. You've done this before.

MARCUS: Army supply officer. I bought a lot of things from a lot of people.

RITA: Fine. Twelve fifty, three months, no setup fee. Deal.

NOAH: Deal. Thank you, Rita.

RITA: I'll send the contract by noon. Read it carefully.

NOAH: Our lawyer would kill us if we didn't.

RITA: Good lawyer. Any other questions?

MARCUS: Waste disposal. Who handles chemical waste?

RITA: We do, every Tuesday. Label everything, with the date and your company name. Unlabeled waste gets you a warning, because if we don't know what it is, we can't dispose of it safely.

MARCUS: And after a warning?

RITA: A very awkward conversation with me.

NOAH: Understood. We'll label everything twice.

RITA: And the meeting room? It's free before nine a.m.

NOAH: Perfect. Our team is meeting there at eight thirty.

RITA: Your whole team? How many?

NOAH: Four co-founders. One of us can't do chemistry, and one is a doctor.

RITA: That sounds like a story. Go. I'll bring extra coffee.

### 장면2 — 첫 스탠드업 미팅, 오전 8시 반

NOAH: Okay, everyone. Our first official stand-up meeting.

ELENA: Why is it called a stand-up?

PRIYA: Because you stand. So it stays short. Fifteen minutes, max.

MARCUS: In the Army, we called it the morning brief.

NOAH: Same idea. Each person answers three questions.

ELENA: Which are?

NOAH: What did you do? What will you do? What's blocking you?

MARCUS: Short and clear. I approve.

NOAH: I'll go first. Done: we're officially a company. We incorporated in Delaware, and all four 83(b) forms are filed, so nobody gets a surprise tax bill on shares they can't sell.

PRIYA: Within thirty days? Diane would be proud.

NOAH: Josh confirmed in writing. Next: I'll build our financial model this week.

ELENA: And what's blocking you?

NOAH: Spreadsheets. Lily reminded me about algebra.

MARCUS: Denise offered to review it. Take the offer.

NOAH: Taking it. Gratefully. Marcus, your turn.

MARCUS: Done: I made a list of contract manufacturers. Twelve in the US.

ELENA: Twelve. That's more than I expected.

MARCUS: Only four do sterile hyaluronic acid gels. Those four matter. The others make creams or devices, and they'd need years to learn what we need, so I crossed them off.

NOAH: Next?

MARCUS: I'll call two of them this week. On my lunch breaks.

PRIYA: Any blockers?

MARCUS: Time. I still work at Corwin until there's a payroll.

NOAH: Understood. Elena?

ELENA: Done: I read the FDA guidance for dermal fillers. Twice.

PRIYA: How long is it?

ELENA: Long enough to need two cups of coffee. Next: I'll talk to three colleagues.

NOAH: About what?

ELENA: How they'd design a filler trial: how many patients, how long to follow them, what to measure. Blocker: I can't share our idea yet, so I have to ask in general terms.

NOAH: Right. Not until we have patents. Priya, last one.

PRIYA: Done: nothing. Next: nothing. Blocker: Halvern owns my brain until further notice.

### 절정·전환 — 방화벽과 외부 시험

ELENA: Priya, that sounds frustrating.

PRIYA: It's torture. I can see the bench. I can't touch it. For four years this idea lived in my head, and now that it finally has a home, I'm not allowed in.

NOAH: Diane was very clear. Talk business, not chemistry.

PRIYA: I know. So this room is a chemistry-free zone for me.

MARCUS: Then we have a problem. The autoclave test.

ELENA: Priya promised three tests, six weeks. Now she can't do them.

NOAH: Can we wait until Priya is free?

MARCUS: We could. But investors will ask, "Does it survive sterilization?" It's the first question anyone serious asks, and if we can't answer it, the meeting is basically over.

ELENA: And "I don't know yet" won't get us money.

NOAH: So what are our options?

MARCUS: Hire an outside lab. A contract research lab.

NOAH: To do what, exactly?

MARCUS: Repeat the recipe from the thesis. Then autoclave it. Then measure. They don't need anything new from Priya, just the public method, so nobody can say Halvern's chemist did the work.

PRIYA: The recipe is public. Page one twelve. Anyone can follow it.

ELENA: Is it okay for you to say that? The page number?

PRIYA: Pointing to a public page, yes. Changing the recipe, no.

NOAH: How much would an outside lab cost?

MARCUS: I asked around. About eighteen thousand. Four to six weeks.

ELENA: Eighteen thousand dollars. From where?

NOAH: From my savings.

PRIYA: Noah, that's a big chunk of your savings.

NOAH: It's the right test. If the gel breaks down in the autoclave, I'd rather lose eighteen thousand dollars now than find out after we've spent millions.

MARCUS: Agreed. Fail fast, fail cheap.

ELENA: And if it passes, it's our first real data.

NOAH: Then it's decided. Marcus, can you own this?

### 마무리 — 정리와 첫날

MARCUS: I own it. I'll get two quotes by Friday, compare price, timeline and quality, and bring you a recommendation. If both labs are bad, I'll find a third.

NOAH: Two quotes. Of course. Two is one.

MARCUS: Now you're learning.

NOAH: Let me summarize the action items. Me: financial model by Thursday.

ELENA: Me: three conversations about trial design. Without revealing the idea.

MARCUS: Me: two lab quotes by Friday. Two manufacturer calls.

PRIYA: Me: check with UT. And read Halvern's conflict policy. Business only.

NOAH: And one more thing. Our company rules.

ELENA: You're writing them on the whiteboard?

NOAH: Rule one: First, do no harm. Rule two: Inside, argue. Outside, one voice.

RITA: Sorry to interrupt. Your contract is signed. Bench fourteen is yours.

NOAH: Thank you, Rita. Our first office. Well, our first bench.

MARCUS: Day one. Mission started.

ELENA: Day one. Let's remember this morning.

PRIYA: I'll remember it. From the other side of the chemistry-free zone.

### ② 비즈니스 컬처 노트 — 스탠드업 미팅(Stand-up Meeting)
애자일(Agile) 개발 문화에서 시작된 짧은 일일 회의다. 서서 진행해 15분 안에 끝내는 것이 원칙이고, 각자 **"What did I do? What will I do? What's blocking me?"** 세 가지만 말한다. 문제 해결 토론은 회의 뒤에 관련자끼리 따로 한다("Let's take that offline."). 미국 스타트업과 테크 기업에서는 개발팀이 아닌 부서도 이 형식을 널리 쓴다.

### ③ 실무 템플릿 — 스탠드업 업데이트 (Stand-up Update)

> **Done:** Yesterday / Last week, I **[completed task]**.
> **Next:** Today / This week, I'll **[planned task]**.
> **Blocker:** I'm blocked by **[issue]**. / No blockers.

예시:
> *Done: I made a list of twelve contract manufacturers. Next: I'll call two of them this week. Blocker: time — I still work full-time at another company.*

### ④ 긴 발화 분석

> **NOAH (장면1):** "Six months is a long time for us. To be honest, we haven't raised money yet, and I don't want to sign for more than we can pay. Is there any flexibility?"

| 구조 | 영어 | 기능 |
|---|---|---|
| ① 문제 제기(부드럽게) | Six months is a long time for us. | 조건을 거절하지 않고 "우리에겐" 부담이라고 완곡하게 말함 |
| ② 솔직한 이유 | To be honest, we haven't raised money yet, | 숨기지 않고 사정을 밝혀 신뢰를 얻음 |
| ③ 원칙(한계선) | and I don't want to sign for more than we can pay. | 감당할 수 있는 범위를 분명히 해 협상의 기준을 세움 |
| ④ 열린 질문 | Is there any flexibility? | 요구 대신 질문으로 상대가 대안을 제시하게 함 |

**활용 패턴:** 부담 표현 → To be honest + 이유 → 한계선 → 열린 질문
**연결 표현:** To be honest(솔직히 말하면) · more than we can…(우리가 ~할 수 있는 것 이상) · Is there any flexibility?(조정 가능할까요?)
**연습:** 1년 약정을 요구하는 거래처에 같은 구조로 30단어 안팎의 조건 조정 요청을 해 보기.

### 장면2 — 첫 스탠드업 미팅, 오전 8시 반

노아: 뭘 했나? 뭘 할 건가? 뭐가 막고 있나?

마커스: 짧고 명확하네. 승인.

노아: 저부터요. 한 일: 우린 공식적으로 회사가 됐어요. 델라웨어에 법인을 설립했고, 네 명 모두 83(b)를 제출했어요. 그러니 팔지도 못하는 지분 때문에 갑자기 세금 고지서를 받는 사람은 없어요.

프리야: 30일 안에? 다이앤이 자랑스러워하겠네.

노아: 조시가 서면으로 확인해 줬어. 다음: 이번 주에 재무 모델을 만들 거야.

엘레나: 그리고 막고 있는 건요?

노아: 스프레드시트요. 릴리가 대수학 얘기를 상기시켜 줬거든요.

마커스: 데니스가 검토해 주겠대. 그 제안 받아.

노아: 받을게요. 감사히. 마커스, 형님 차례요.

마커스: 한 일: 위탁생산업체 목록 만들었어. 미국에 열두 곳.

엘레나: 열두 곳이요. 생각보다 많네요.

마커스: 무균 히알루론산 젤을 하는 데는 네 곳뿐이야. 그 네 곳이 중요해. 나머지는 크림이나 기기를 만들어서, 우리한테 필요한 걸 배우려면 몇 년은 걸릴 거야. 그래서 지웠어.

노아: 다음은요?

마커스: 이번 주에 두 곳에 전화할 거야. 점심시간에.

프리야: 막힌 거 있어요?

마커스: 시간. 월급 나올 때까진 아직 코윈에서 일하니까.

노아: 알겠어요. 엘레나?

엘레나: 한 일: 피부 필러에 대한 FDA 가이드라인을 읽었어요. 두 번.

프리야: 얼마나 길어요?

엘레나: 커피 두 잔이 필요할 만큼요. 다음: 동료 세 명과 얘기해 볼게요.

노아: 무슨 얘기요?

엘레나: 필러 임상을 어떻게 설계할지요. 환자는 몇 명, 얼마나 오래 추적할지, 무엇을 측정할지. 막힌 점: 아직 우리 아이디어를 공유할 수 없어서, 일반적인 질문으로만 물어봐야 해요.

노아: 맞아요. 특허 전까지는 안 돼요. 프리야, 마지막.

프리야: 한 일: 없음. 다음: 없음. 막힌 점: 추후 통지가 있을 때까지 할번이 내 뇌를 소유함.

### 절정·전환 — 방화벽과 외부 시험

엘레나: 프리야, 정말 답답하겠어요.

프리야: 고문이에요. 실험대가 보이는데 만질 수가 없어요. 4년 동안 이 아이디어는 제 머릿속에만 있었는데, 이제 드디어 집이 생겼더니 정작 저는 들어갈 수가 없네요.

노아: 다이앤이 아주 분명하게 말했잖아. 사업 얘기는 되고, 화학은 안 된다고.

프리야: 알아. 그러니까 이 방은 나한텐 화학 금지 구역이야.

마커스: 그럼 문제가 있어. 고압 멸균 시험.

엘레나: 프리야가 시험 세 가지, 6주를 약속했죠. 그런데 이제 할 수 없어요.

노아: 프리야가 자유로워질 때까지 기다릴 수 있을까요?

마커스: 그럴 수도 있지. 근데 투자자들이 물을 거야. "멸균을 견디나요?" 진지한 사람이라면 누구나 제일 먼저 묻는 질문이고, 거기에 답 못 하면 미팅은 사실상 끝이야.

엘레나: 그리고 "아직 모른다"로는 돈을 못 받을 거고요.

노아: 그럼 선택지가 뭐가 있죠?

마커스: 외부 연구소를 고용하는 거야. 수탁 연구소.

노아: 정확히 뭘 하게요?

마커스: 논문에 있는 제조법을 그대로 재현하고, 멸균하고, 측정하는 거지. 프리야한테서 새로운 건 하나도 필요 없고 공개된 방법만 쓰면 돼. 그러면 할번 소속 화학자가 일했다고 아무도 말 못 해.

프리야: 그 제조법은 공개돼 있어. 112페이지. 누구나 따라 할 수 있어.

엘레나: 그 말은 해도 괜찮아요? 페이지 번호요?

프리야: 공개된 페이지를 가리키는 건 괜찮아요. 제조법을 바꾸는 건 안 되고요.

노아: 외부 연구소 비용은 얼마나 들어요?

마커스: 수소문해 봤어. 1만 8천 달러쯤. 4주에서 6주.

엘레나: 1만 8천 달러. 어디서요?

노아: 제 저축에서요.

프리야: 노아, 그거 네 저축에서 큰 부분이잖아.

노아: 꼭 필요한 시험이야. 젤이 고압 멸균기에서 분해된다면, 수백만 달러를 쓴 뒤에 알게 되느니 지금 1만 8천 달러를 잃는 게 나아.

마커스: 동의. 빨리 실패하고, 싸게 실패하자.

엘레나: 그리고 통과하면 우리의 첫 진짜 데이터가 되죠.

노아: 그럼 결정이에요. 마커스, 이거 맡아 줄 수 있어요?
