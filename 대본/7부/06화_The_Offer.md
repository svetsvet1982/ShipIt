## 대본

### 도입 — 오후 7시, Larkspur 별실

TOM: Noah. Right on time. That's new. You used to be late to everything.

NOAH: Hi, Tom. My fiancée has a spreadsheet. I'm home by ten.

TOM: Fiancée. Congratulations. Claire, right? The engineer. Dallas, the pecan pie.

NOAH: …How do you know about the pie?

TOM: Austin is a village. With better barbecue. Sit. I ordered the wine. You'll like it. You won't admit it.

NOAH: Congratulations on your promotion. President of Halvern Aesthetics. I read it in January.

TOM: Thank you. The view from the top floor is nice. The air is thinner.

NOAH: I saw your booth in Seoul. Forty square meters. Next to the coffee cart.

TOM: And I saw yours. One hundred square meters in the main hall, with Lily's logo four meters high. I stood there for a while, if you want the truth, because I remembered the kid who couldn't get a meeting with anybody. That kid built that booth.

NOAH: You were in Seoul? I didn't see you.

TOM: You weren't supposed to. I also watched your guy in Parliament. Okafor. Very good. I tried to hire him once.

NOAH: He told me. He said no in one sentence. His mother's influence.

TOM: Everybody's mother. Okay. Small talk is over. I'm not good at it, and you're on a curfew.

NOAH: Before you start, one ground rule. Whatever you say tonight, I won't answer tonight, because anything this big has to go through my co-founders and the board first. So I'll listen, I'll ask questions, and I'll take notes. That's all.

TOM: Good. Then listen carefully. Because I'm only going to say it once.

### 장면1 — 제안

TOM: Halvern wants to acquire Veyra. All of it. One point two billion dollars.

NOAH: …

TOM: I'm always honest with you. I'm just not always kind.

NOAH: And there's another reason, and I think it matters more. Your CEO retires next year, and you want the job. A big acquisition in a growing market is exactly what a board likes to see from a candidate. So this isn't only about Lumaderm.

NOAH: Page six. "Brennan, a leading candidate." Buying Veyra is your ticket.

WAITER: Excuse me, gentlemen. The reserve, as you requested, Mr. Brennan.

NOAH: That's a good line. Did you practise it?

TOM: Twice. In the car. It sounded better in the car.

NOAH: Tom, can I ask some questions? Specific ones?

TOM: That's why I booked the private room.

NOAH: Thank you. I'm going to write your answers on a napkin. Old habit.

TOM: The famous napkin. Go ahead. I'll speak slowly.

NOAH: Question one. We sell only to medical professionals. In every country. Does that rule survive?

### 장면2 — 냅킨 위의 다섯 가지 답

TOM: That rule will be reviewed as part of integration.

NOAH: "Reviewed as part of integration." In British, that's "no." In Halvern, too?

NOAH: A well-paid voice. Without a veto.

TOM: Without a veto. With eight million dollars. She can buy a lot of vetoes with that.

NOAH: You don't know Elena.

TOM: I know she walked out of a meeting once. Over a medical assistant in Houston. I respected that.

NOAH: Question four. Korea. We're in talks with Daeyang Bio. What happens to that?

TOM: Daeyang is our syringe supplier. A joint venture between our supplier and our subsidiary? Conflict of interest. It ends.

NOAH: Their supply contract with you ends next year.

TOM: …You're better informed than I thought. Ms. Han talks to you.

NOAH: I didn't say that.

TOM: You didn't have to. Contracts get renewed, Noah. Especially when the alternative is losing Halvern.

NOAH: Question five. Timing. What do you need from me?

TOM: An answer in twenty-one days. Then forty-five days of exclusivity. No-shop. You don't talk to other buyers.

NOAH: Or other partners? Like Daeyang? Or Tokyo?

TOM: Tokyo. Mitsukawa. Ishikawa is a smart man. He'll wait to see what you decide.

NOAH: You know about Tokyo too.

TOM: Two floors below Mitsukawa, kid. Shimbashi has good bars.

NOAH: Let me read it back, so we don't misunderstand each other later. Five answers. Rule, reviewed. Committee, advisory. Veto, gone. Korea, ends. And you want an answer in twenty-one days, followed by forty-five days of no-shop. Is that right?

TOM: You make it sound bad. You left out the one point two billion.

NOAH: It's on the napkin. At the top. Underlined.

### 절정·전환 — 오후 9시, 디저트

TOM: Let me tell you what happens if you say no. Not a threat. A forecast.

NOAH: Go on.

TOM: Lumaderm Flex launches in Korea this fall, at Bellacare prices. We can lose money there for three years without our shareholders noticing, because Korea is a rounding error for us. For you, it's your growth story. Can you lose money for three years?

NOAH: That sounds like a threat, Tom.

TOM: It's a business plan. With your name on it, if you say no.

NOAH: Can I ask you something personal?

TOM: You're going to anyway.

NOAH: When I left Halvern, you said I'd be back in a year. Begging. Do you remember?

TOM: I remember. I was wrong. I'm rarely wrong. It bothered me for a long time.

NOAH: Is that why you're here? To be right, finally?

TOM: I hired you at twenty-six. You couldn't read a P and L, you couldn't run a meeting, and you argued with every regional director in the building. Everything you used against me in that lawsuit, every tactic, I taught you. Don't forget that.

NOAH: You did. I've never denied it.

TOM: Then let me finish what I started. Bring you home. With a fortune. Nothing personal. It's just business.

NOAH: …It's personal to me, Tom. It always was. Since the emergency room.

TOM: I know. That's what makes you good. And that's what will make you poor.

NOAH: Thank you for dinner, Tom. And for the offer. I'll take it to the board.

TOM: Not "no"? Not "never"?

NOAH: I have a fiduciary duty to our shareholders, Tom, and investors who expect to hear about any offer within forty-eight hours. If I say 'no' at a dinner table without asking anyone, I'm not protecting the company. I'm protecting my ego. I'll do this properly.

TOM: Properly. Look at you. All grown up. Written offer, tomorrow at nine. Twenty-one days.

NOAH: Understood. Twenty-one days.

TOM: And Noah. The wine. You liked it, didn't you?

NOAH: …It was very good.

TOM: I knew it. You won't admit you like things that come from me.

NOAH: Good night, Tom.

TOM: Good night, kid. The clock starts at nine.

### ② 비즈니스 컬처 노트 — 인수 제안을 받을 때
인수 제안의 핵심은 **총액이 아니라 구조**다. 클로징 현금과 **언아웃**(성과 연동 후불)은 위험이 다르다. 언아웃 마일스톤이 인수자의 경영 아래 달성되어야 한다면, 그 달성 여부는 매도자가 통제할 수 없다. **잔류 패키지**와 **스톡옵션 가속**은 직원에게 큰 혜택이지만, 협상 테이블에서는 감정적 압박으로도 작동한다. 이런 자리에서는 ① **즉답하지 않는다**고 미리 선언하고, ② **구체적 확인 질문**으로 원칙(판매 원칙, 안전위원회, 거부권, 기존 제휴)이 인수 후 어떻게 되는지 드러내며, ③ 들은 내용을 **요약해 되묻고(냅킨 요약)**, ④ 신인의무에 따라 **이사회와 투자자에게 정해진 절차대로** 알린다. "reviewed as part of integration", "advisory" 같은 표현은 대개 **현재의 권한이 사라진다**는 뜻이다.

| 구조 | 영어 | 기능 |
|---|---|---|
| ① 근거(의무) | I have a fiduciary duty to our shareholders, Tom, and investors who expect to hear about any offer within forty-eight hours. | 즉답하지 않는 이유를 개인 감정이 아닌 공식 의무로 제시 |
| ② 가정(반대 상황) | If I say 'no' at a dinner table without asking anyone, | 즉석 거절이 어떤 모습인지 그려 보여 줌 |
| ③ 대비(재정의) | I'm not protecting the company. I'm protecting my ego. | "A가 아니라 B"로 즉답의 진짜 성격을 한 줄로 압축 |

**연습:** 거래처가 회식 자리에서 계약 조건 변경에 즉답을 요구할 때, 같은 구조로 40단어 안팎으로 정중하게 답을 미뤄 보기.
