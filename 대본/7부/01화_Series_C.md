## 대본

### 도입 — 오전 10시, 허드슨 야드

VICTOR: Noah. Welcome to Bluewater. I've read your deck twice. Rebecca has read it four times. She has questions.

REBECCA: Twenty-three, to be precise. I've prioritised them. Don't worry.

NOAH: Thanks for having us. Paul Novak, our CFO. Dr. Priya Raman, CTO. Dr. Elena Ruiz, Chief Medical Officer.

PAUL: And Richard Hale is joining by video. Our lead board member since the seed round.

RICHARD: Morning, Victor. Go easy on them. Or don't. They're better when they're pushed.

VICTOR: We don't go easy. We go thorough. It's a crossover fund, which means we invest privately now and then keep holding through the IPO and beyond. So if there's a weakness in your story, I'd much rather find it in this room than read about it later in an analyst report.

PRIYA: So you're asking the questions public investors will ask. Eighteen months early.

VICTOR: Exactly. If you can't answer us, you can't answer them.

NOAH: Then let's start. We're raising one hundred fifty million. At an eight hundred fifty million pre-money valuation.

REBECCA: That's roughly sixteen times trailing revenue. Ambitious.

PAUL: Sixteen times trailing, yes, but about ten times forward, and forward is the multiple that matters for a company growing this fast. We grew fifty-eight percent last year, and our gross margin is eighty-six percent, so this isn't growth we're buying at any cost.

VICTOR: Growth gets you the meeting. It doesn't get you the price. Walk us through it.

NOAH: Here's the thing. Three questions matter. Where the money goes. When we make money. And why nobody can copy us.

REBECCA: Use of proceeds. Path to profitability. Defensible moat. My first three questions.

NOAH: Then we've saved you some time.

### 장면1 — 자금 사용과 수익성 경로

PAUL: Use of proceeds. Asia, sixty million. Manufacturing, thirty-five. New products, thirty. IPO readiness and working capital, twenty-five.

REBECCA: Sixty million for Asia. Before you've sold a single syringe there. Why so much? Why now?

NOAH: Because Asia is the largest filler market in the world, and it's moving fast. Clinics there are choosing their long-term suppliers right now, and once those relationships are set, they're very hard to break. Late entrants pay double, in marketing and in time, so waiting would actually cost us more.

REBECCA: Korea is also the most competitive. Dozens of domestic fillers. At half your price. Some lower.

NOAH: Correct. We won't win on price. We'll win where price isn't the deciding factor.

REBECCA: Which is where, exactly?

ELENA: Safety-led clinics. Doctors who've treated complications from cheap fillers. They exist in every market. Including Korea.

REBECCA: How big is that segment? In numbers. Not in conviction.

PAUL: In Korea, the premium segment is roughly eighteen percent of volume. And thirty-five percent of value.

VICTOR: And your source?

PAUL: A third-party market study. And interviews with forty Seoul clinics. Grace Kim ran them. Appendix C.

REBECCA: I read Appendix C. It was the best part of the deck. Who's Grace?

NOAH: Our Head of Analytics. Korean-American. She'll lead our Korean launch team.

VICTOR: Path to profitability. You turned EBITDA-positive in the fourth quarter. Is that sustainable? Or seasonal?

PAUL: Sustainable in the US and the UK. Asia will push us back into losses. For about six quarters.

VICTOR: You're telling me you'll lose money on purpose.

PAUL: I'm telling you exactly how much, and for how long, which is very different from losing money by accident. The US and the UK keep funding themselves, Asia absorbs losses for about six quarters while we build the market, and then we reach group-level profitability in year nine. That's the number.

REBECCA: Customer acquisition cost. What's your payback period?

PAUL: Eleven months in the US. Fourteen in the UK, nine after the reframe. Korea will be longer. Maybe eighteen.

REBECCA: Your UK launch missed its first quarter by forty-five percent. Why should I trust your Korea forecast?

NOAH: Fair question. Honestly, you shouldn't trust the forecast. You should trust how we respond when it's wrong.

REBECCA: Go on.

NOAH: We ran a blameless post-mortem in week twelve, because I didn't want anyone hiding mistakes to protect themselves. We found the root cause, rebuilt the message, and sales quadrupled in eight weeks. The forecast was wrong, but the response was fast, and that's the part you can actually rely on.

REBECCA: And the root cause?

NOAH: Me. I imported the US playbook because I was in a hurry. I won't make that mistake in Korea.

VICTOR: A CEO who names himself as the root cause. That's rarer than EBITDA.

RICHARD: I've been telling you, Victor. He's annoyingly honest.

REBECCA: Thirty million for "new products." That's vague. What exactly?

PRIYA: Two programmes. The first is a skin booster using FlexLink chemistry, so we're building on technology we already own and understand. The second is early work on a neurotoxin, through a partner, because we don't have that expertise in-house, and pretending otherwise would simply waste your money.

REBECCA: Which partner?

PRIYA: We're in early conversations. I can't name them. Yet.

VICTOR: Korean?

NOAH: I'd prefer not to speculate in a room with lawyers' ears. But you'll be the first to know.

RICHARD: Victor, he means yes. In founder.

NOAH: Richard. You're not helping.

### 장면2 — 해자, 그리고 그 질문

VICTOR: Now the most important question. What's your defensible moat? Why can't Halvern copy you in two years?

PRIYA: Layer one. FlexLink. Patented until twenty forty-one. The dissolution profile can't be matched without infringing.

REBECCA: Patents get designed around. Every chemist in Halvern is trying right now.

PRIYA: I know. I used to be one of them. That's why layer one isn't enough.

ELENA: Layer two. The safety system. Twelve thousand certified injectors. Serial scans. A registry of every vascular event.

REBECCA: Why is that a moat? Halvern could build a training programme.

ELENA: They could, and they probably will. But our registry gets better with every injection, because every vascular event teaches us something about prevention and treatment. That's six years of real-world data from twelve thousand certified injectors. A competitor can copy a training manual in a month, but you can't buy six years.

VICTOR: A network effect. In medical safety. Interesting.

NOAH: And layer three. The hardest to copy. We sell only to medical professionals. Every syringe. Traced.

REBECCA: That's a restriction. Not a moat.

NOAH: Here's the thing. To copy it, Halvern would have to stop selling to beauticians, and in the UK, that's sixty percent of their volume. So the question isn't whether they could do it technically. It's whether their shareholders would accept losing most of that business just to imitate us, and I don't think they would.

VICTOR: So your moat is a rule your competitor can't afford to follow.

NOAH: Exactly. Our rule is our brand. And our brand costs them money to imitate.

REBECCA: …That's a better answer than I expected. I'm crossing out question nine.

VICTOR: Then let me ask the question nobody in this room wants. The Financial Ledger. December. "Halvern exploring a bid."

NOAH: I read it. Like everyone else.

VICTOR: Are you for sale, Noah?

NOAH: We're not running a process. Nobody has made an offer. But my job is to listen to anything that's serious.

VICTOR: That's a careful answer.

NOAH: It's a true answer. If I said "never," you'd worry I was ignoring my fiduciary duty to our shareholders, which would include you, if this round closes. The board and I owe everyone a fair hearing of any serious offer. Listening isn't the same as wanting to sell, and I'd rather be precise than reassuring.

RICHARD: And if he said "maybe," you'd worry he wanted to cash out. He found the narrow path.

VICTOR: Has Tom Brennan contacted you?

NOAH: He's invited me to dinner. Twice. I postponed. February. Then March.

REBECCA: Why postpone?

NOAH: Because I wanted to close this round first. A company with a Series C negotiates from strength.

VICTOR: So you are thinking about it.

NOAH: I'm thinking about leverage. Not about selling. There's a difference.

PRIYA: Can I add something? As a founder?

VICTOR: Please.

PRIYA: I left Halvern because they wouldn't let me build this. I asked more than once, I showed them the data, and the answer was always no. So I walked out and built it somewhere else. I didn't spend all these years on it just to hand it back to the people who said no.

### 절정·전환 — 오후 1시, 조건

VICTOR: Thank you. All of you. Rebecca and I will step out. Ten minutes.

PAUL: Ten minutes in a VC office. That's either very good or very bad.

ELENA: Priya. That last line. Did you plan it?

PRIYA: No. Victor looked at Noah like he was a price tag. I didn't like it.

RICHARD: For the record, I liked it. Investors need to see conviction. Not just numbers.

NOAH: Richard, if Halvern does make an offer, where will you stand?

RICHARD: Wherever the return is. That's my job, and I've never pretended otherwise. You knew that in the seed round. But I'll say this too: a serious offer would have to beat what this company can become on its own, and after today, that bar just got a lot higher.

NOAH: I did. I just wanted to hear you say it out loud.

VICTOR: We're back. We'd like to lead. One hundred fifty million. But not at eight-fifty.

PAUL: What's the number?

VICTOR: Seven hundred eighty pre-money. Asia adds risk. And six quarters of losses.

NOAH: We anchored at eight-fifty for a reason. The UK proved we can recover from a bad quarter in eight weeks, which is exactly the risk you're pricing in Korea. You've already seen what happens when one of our launches goes wrong. We find the cause, we fix it, and we grow faster afterwards.

VICTOR: And Korea could take eight quarters. I'm pricing the uncertainty.

PAUL: Eight-twenty. With a clean structure. No ratchets. No guaranteed returns.

REBECCA: Eight-twenty, clean. If the Asia plan has a partner signed by the third quarter.

VICTOR: And two more conditions. A board seat for me. And IPO readiness within twenty-four months.

RICHARD: Standard. Reasonable. I'd support both.

VICTOR: And one last thing. If Halvern makes a serious offer, we expect to hear about it within forty-eight hours.

NOAH: Agreed. In writing. Our lawyer insists on writing.

VICTOR: Then we have a deal. Subject to diligence. Term sheet by Friday.

NOAH: Thank you, Victor. Rebecca. You made us better today.

REBECCA: You still owe me answers to fourteen questions. By email.

PAUL: You'll have them Thursday. With appendices.

REBECCA: Appendix C quality, please.

PAUL: I'll tell Grace. She'll be insufferable.

### 마무리 — 오후 6시, 라과디아 공항 라운지

ELENA: Eight-twenty. Clean. One hundred fifty million. How do you feel?

NOAH: Relieved, mostly. And slightly afraid, to be honest. Today was about making promises, and now we actually have to deliver Asia, with a partner signed by the third quarter. For years, raising money felt like the finish line. Now it just feels like the starting gun.

PRIYA: Partner signed by Q3. That means Seoul. That means Daeyang. Or Seorin. Or both.

ELENA: Mr. Park has a first-negotiation right for Asia. In writing, since year three. Before anyone gets excited about Daeyang, remember that Seorin trusted us when we were small. First, do no harm. That applies to partnerships as much as it applies to patients.

NOAH: I know. I haven't forgotten. We call Seorin before we meet anyone else.

PAUL: Noah. Email. Han Seo-yeon. Subject line: "Asia Aesthetic Congress — confirmation."

NOAH: Read it. Please. I'm off the clock in fourteen minutes.

PAUL: "Main hall booth, one hundred square meters, confirmed. March twelfth. Shall we meet on the second day? Respectfully, Han Seo-yeon."

ELENA: One hundred square meters. Paris was ten.

PRIYA: We've come a long way since Paris. And a long way from the lab.

### ② 비즈니스 컬처 노트 — IPO 전 대형 라운드(크로스오버)의 질문
크로스오버 투자자는 **상장 이후 공개 시장 투자자가 할 질문을 미리** 던진다. 핵심은 세 가지다. ① **자금 사용 계획(use of proceeds)**: 금액과 목적을 숫자로. ② **수익성 경로(path to profitability)**: 언제까지 얼마의 손실을 감수하고, 언제 흑자인지 구체적으로. ③ **방어 가능한 해자(defensible moat)**: 특허처럼 시간이 지나면 약해지는 것과, 데이터·네트워크·브랜드처럼 시간이 지날수록 강해지는 것을 구분해 설명한다. 인수설 같은 민감한 질문에는 "절대 안 판다"도 "생각 중이다"도 아닌, **신인의무를 인정하는 정확한 표현("We're not running a process… my job is to listen to anything that's serious")**이 표준이다. 가치 평가 협상에서는 **앵커(첫 제시 가격)의 근거**를 설명하고, 가격 대신 **구조(래칫·수익 보장 없음)**로 균형을 맞추기도 한다.

### ④ 긴 발화 분석

> **NOAH (장면2):** "Here's the thing. To copy it, Halvern would have to stop selling to beauticians, and in the UK, that's sixty percent of their volume. So the question isn't whether they could do it technically. It's whether their shareholders would accept losing most of that business just to imitate us, and I don't think they would."

| 구조 | 영어 | 기능 |
|---|---|---|
| ① 신호(주의 집중) | Here's the thing. | 반론에 답하기 전에 핵심이 온다는 신호 |
| ② 근거(조건 + 숫자) | To copy it, Halvern would have to stop selling to beauticians, and in the UK, that's sixty percent of their volume. | "따라 하려면 ~해야 한다"로 모방 비용을 숫자로 제시 |
| ③ 논점 재설정(대비) | So the question isn't whether they could do it technically. It's whether their shareholders would accept… | "A가 문제가 아니라 B가 문제"로 상대 질문의 틀을 바꿈 |
| ④ 판단(의견) | and I don't think they would. | 짧은 개인 판단으로 마무리 |

**활용 패턴:** Here's the thing → 모방하려면 치러야 할 비용(숫자) → "The question isn't A. It's B." → 내 판단
**연결 표현:** would have to(~해야만 할 것이다) · So the question isn't…(그러니 문제는 ~가 아니다) · just to(단지 ~하려고)
**연습:** "경쟁사가 우리 서비스를 금방 베끼지 않을까요?"라는 투자자 질문에 같은 구조로 40~50단어로 답해 보기.
