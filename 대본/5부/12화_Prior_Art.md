### 도입 — 오전 8시 30분, 조정 센터 로비

PRIYA: Four boxes. Scanned twice. Marcus insisted. My mother made us sandwiches for the drive.

RAYMOND: And the one item I asked about? From the conference box?

PRIYA: Here. Original. In a plastic sleeve. I didn't even know I'd kept it.

NOAH: What is it?

RAYMOND: Later. Not yet. Some cards stay hidden until the right moment.

DIANE: Rules for today. This is mediation, not court, so nobody hands down a verdict and nobody wins. Either everybody settles and walks out with something they can live with, or we don't, and we go back to spending money on lawyers. Get it in writing either way.

RAYMOND: Everything said today is "without prejudice." It can't be used in court later.

NOAH: So we can speak freely?

DIANE: Freely. But carefully. "Without prejudice" doesn't mean "without memory."

PRIYA: Who's the mediator?

RAYMOND: Judge Robert Kwan. Retired federal judge. Twenty years on patent cases. He's fair. And impatient.

NOAH: What does Halvern want today?

RAYMOND: Money. Or a royalty. And most of all, they want our patent office challenge to disappear.

PRIYA: Why does that matter so much to them?

RAYMOND: Because if the patent office kills claim seven, Halvern loses it against everyone, not just us. Every competitor who uses that chemistry would suddenly be free to sell. So for Halvern, this isn't a fight about one small company in Austin. It's about protecting the whole market.

### 장면1 — 오전 9시, 공동 세션

KWAN: Good morning. I'm Judge Kwan. I've read both briefs. Twice. I don't like long speeches.

DIANE: Understood, Judge. We'll be short.

KWAN: Each side, five minutes. Then separate rooms. I'll walk between you. Halvern first.

PATRICIA: Patricia Vance, general counsel. Halvern invented enzyme-responsive filler technology. We patented it. Veyra uses it.

TOM: We're open to a license, because we'd rather do business than spend three years in court. Twelve percent of sales, and they withdraw their patent office challenge. That's a fair price for using technology we paid to develop. Nothing personal, Noah. It's just business.

KWAN: Thank you. Veyra. Five minutes.

RAYMOND: Raymond Ostrowski. Two points. First, Veyra Fill uses a different crosslinker. Claim one doesn't apply.

NOAH: Second, claim seven is invalid. Our CTO, Dr. Priya Raman, published it six years before Halvern filed.

PATRICIA: With respect, it's a PhD thesis. In vitro experiments, done in test tubes in a university lab. That's not a filler, and it's not a product. There's a very long road between an interesting result on paper and something a doctor can actually inject into a patient.

KWAN: Dr. Raman? Would you like to respond? Briefly.

PRIYA: Page one hundred twelve. A full method for an injectable gel. Crosslinked. Enzyme-sensitive.

RAYMOND: Page one-forty. Injectability through a twenty-seven-gauge needle. Measured. With force data.

PRIYA: Page one-fifty-three. Dissolution two point four times faster with hyaluronidase. In a filler-like gel.

KWAN: That sounds like more than test tubes.

PATRICIA: A thesis sitting in a library is not the same as a commercial invention.

RAYMOND: Patent law disagrees. Public is public. A library is public.

TOM: Priya. You worked at Halvern for four years. Did you share this work with our team?

DIANE: Objection. This is mediation, Tom. Not a deposition.

KWAN: Ms. Foster is right. But I'll allow Dr. Raman to answer. If she wants.

PRIYA: I finished my thesis, and four years later I joined Halvern. I never worked on fillers there, not for a single day. And your patent was filed in my second year, by a team I wasn't part of.

TOM: And you never shared your thesis with our team?

PRIYA: I didn't need to. It was already in the library.

KWAN: Thank you. Separate rooms now. Halvern in Room A. Veyra in Room B. I'll start with Veyra.

NOAH: Priya. That was incredible.

PRIYA: My hands are shaking. Is that normal?

DIANE: Completely normal. Mine shake too. I just hide them under the table.

KWAN: Room B. Let's talk honestly. Without prejudice. What do you really want?

NOAH: To stop fighting. To sell our product. And to stop paying lawyers. No offense, Diane.

DIANE: None taken. I'm expensive.

KWAN: Would you pay a royalty? Any royalty?

NOAH: Not for an idea my CTO published first. That's a principle. Not a negotiating position.

KWAN: Principles are expensive in court. Your patent office challenge could take eighteen months, and a full trial could take three years. During all that time, your customers will be reading about a lawsuit instead of your product. I'm not saying give up. I'm saying count the cost.

RAYMOND: Judge, our prior art is strong. You saw page one-fifty-three.

KWAN: I did. It's strong. Not perfect. Halvern will argue a thesis isn't "enabling." That's a real argument.

RAYMOND: Then perhaps it's time for the sleeve.

### 장면2 — 슬리브 속의 종이

KWAN: The sleeve?

RAYMOND: Priya. Show the judge.

PRIYA: A sign-in sheet. From my poster session. At the Biomaterials Conference. Twelve years ago.

KWAN: A sign-in sheet? Why does that matter?

RAYMOND: Look at line fourteen. The name. And the company.

KWAN: "Dr. Alan Wu. Halvern Research." …Alan Wu.

RAYMOND: He's the first inventor named on Halvern's patent. According to this sheet, he stood in front of Priya's poster and signed his name, six years before he filed. In other words, the man who claims he invented this had already seen it on a conference wall.

NOAH: …He saw it. He actually saw it.

PRIYA: He asked me three questions. I remember now. About the enzyme. He wrote everything down.

KWAN: That changes things. Significantly.

DIANE: It doesn't prove copying. But it proves Halvern knew. Long before filing.

RAYMOND: And a patent office examiner would want to know. So would a jury.

KWAN: Do I have permission to show this to Halvern? In confidence?

NOAH: Before you do. Judge, I want to be clear about one thing.

KWAN: Go on.

NOAH: We're not here to humiliate Halvern, or Dr. Wu. He may have learned from Priya's work, or he may not have; that's not our fight today. We want peace, not a headline. If this sheet ends up in the newspapers, nobody in this building really wins.

KWAN: That's unusual. Most parties want both.

NOAH: We've had enough headlines this year.

KWAN: Then tell me your terms. If Halvern wants peace too.

RAYMOND: One. Halvern drops the lawsuit. Two. A royalty-free license to the '847 patent. Forever.

NOAH: Three. We withdraw our patent office challenge. They keep their patent. Against others.

PRIYA: Wait. We let them keep claim seven? After this?

NOAH: Priya, our goal is to sell safely and keep our customers thinking about patients, not court dates. It's not to destroy their patent. If claim seven survives, that's Halvern's problem with other companies, not ours. Let them fight everyone else while we get back to work.

PRIYA: …That's very calm. For you.

NOAH: I learned it from Miriam. Ask for what you need. Not what you can take.

DIANE: Four. Halvern covers our legal costs. Two and a half million.

RAYMOND: Five. Mutual non-disparagement. Neither side talks badly about the other's patents. Publicly.

KWAN: Anything Halvern gets? Besides peace?

PRIYA: Our sterilization process patent. They've been trying to solve that problem for years. They can license it.

NOAH: At a fair royalty. Three percent. It's a cross-license. Both sides give. Both sides get.

### 절정·전환 — 오후, 셔틀 협상

KWAN: I'm going to Room A. With the sheet. And your terms. Wait here.

DIANE: Now we wait. The hardest part of mediation.

MARCUS: Noah. I'm on the phone. How's it going?

NOAH: Priya found a sign-in sheet. From twelve years ago. With Halvern's inventor's name.

MARCUS: In the garage? In box three?

PRIYA: Box three. You scanned it twice.

MARCUS: Two is one. Told you.

RAYMOND: Forty minutes. He's still in there. That's a good sign. Bad news is fast.

KWAN: I'm back. Halvern saw the sheet. Ms. Vance asked for ten minutes. Alone with Mr. Brennan.

NOAH: And?

KWAN: They accept the drop, the license, and the withdrawal, which is the hard part, honestly. Where they're pushing back is money. They want the sterilization license at two percent, not three.

DIANE: And legal costs?

KWAN: One and a half million. Not two and a half.

RAYMOND: Splitting the difference. Classic.

NOAH: Judge, can I say what matters to us? So you can carry it back?

KWAN: Please.

NOAH: The license and the peace matter most. The money matters less. But the royalty is about respect.

DIANE: Three percent on our process. Legal costs, two and a half million. Or two.

KWAN: A range. That's helpful. Most people give me walls. Room A again. Twenty minutes.

PRIYA: Noah, why give a range? Why not hold at two and a half?

NOAH: Because the judge needs room to move. If we give him walls, he can't build a bridge, and then we're back in court for three years. A range tells him what matters to us and where we can bend. Here's the thing: half a million isn't worth losing the deal.

DIANE: Your father would say that.

MARCUS: "Build the bridge you can finish." I remember. Good luck, all of you.

KWAN: Done. Three percent. Two million in legal costs. Signed today. Both sides.

RAYMOND: Settled out of court. In one day. Judge, I'm impressed.

### ② 비즈니스 컬처 노트 — 특허 분쟁 조정
미국 특허 소송은 비용과 기간이 커서 많은 사건이 **조정(mediation)**으로 끝난다. 조정에서는 양측이 공동 세션 후 **별실(caucus)**로 나뉘고, 조정인이 오가며 제안을 전한다. 대화는 보통 **"without prejudice"** 조건이라 법정 증거로 쓸 수 없지만, 상대가 들은 내용을 잊는 것은 아니다. 강한 증거는 **맞는 순간**에 공개해야 효과가 크다. 좋은 협상가는 조정인에게 **벽(고정 숫자)이 아니라 범위**를 주어 합의 공간을 만든다. 특허 분쟁은 흔히 **크로스 라이선스**(서로의 특허 사용 허락)와 비용 분담, 소송·심판 취하, 비방 금지 조항으로 마무리된다.

### ④ 긴 발화 분석

> **NOAH (절정·전환):** "Because the judge needs room to move. If we give him walls, he can't build a bridge, and then we're back in court for three years. A range tells him what matters to us and where we can bend. Here's the thing: half a million isn't worth losing the deal."

| 구조 | 영어 | 기능 |
|---|---|---|
| ① 원칙(이유) | Because the judge needs room to move. | 질문(왜 범위를 주나?)에 한 문장으로 답한다 |
| ② 비유+결과(조건문) | If we give him walls, he can't build a bridge, and then we're back in court for three years. | 고정 숫자의 위험을 그림과 구체적 결과로 보여 준다 |
| ③ 기능 설명 | A range tells him what matters to us and where we can bend. | 범위가 상대에게 주는 정보를 설명 |
| ④ 결론(우선순위) | Here's the thing: half a million isn't worth losing the deal. | 작은 돈과 큰 목표를 비교해 판단 기준을 제시 |

**활용 패턴:** 원칙 → If…, then(나쁜 결과) → 대안의 효과 → "X isn't worth losing Y."
**연결 표현:** room to move(움직일 여지) · and then(그러면 결국) · where we can bend(양보할 수 있는 지점) · isn't worth losing(잃을 만한 가치가 없다)
**연습:** 협상에서 왜 고정 가격 대신 가격 범위를 제시하는지 팀원에게 40~50단어로 설명해 보기.
