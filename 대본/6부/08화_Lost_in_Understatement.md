## 대본

### 도입 — 밤 8시, 클래펌의 펍

SARAH: Noah! Over here. By the fire. I've saved you three seats. Fought for them.

NOAH: Sarah, thank you for coming. On a Thursday night. At an hour's notice.

SARAH: Your text said "Emergency. British." I couldn't resist. This is James.

JAMES: James Okafor. Sarah said there's an American in trouble. I came for the entertainment.

ELENA: Elena Ruiz. Thank you. The American is him. I'm Mexican-American. I'm only half in trouble.

SOFIA: Sofia Alvarez. Sales. I'm the one who thought it went well.

JAMES: Ah. One of those days. What are we drinking?

NOAH: Whatever you recommend. I clearly can't be trusted with British decisions today.

JAMES: A pint of bitter, then. It matches the mood.

SARAH: James was an A and E nurse for twelve years, so nothing frightens him. Then he trained injectors for years. If anyone can translate Oliver for you, it's him, because he knows everyone in this industry, and everyone knows him.

JAMES: Everyone knows everyone. London's a village with a tube map.

ELENA: Do you know Oliver Grant?

JAMES: Everyone knows Oliver. Lovely man. Excellent tea. Never says no.

SOFIA: That's what we thought! He never said no.

JAMES: No. He never says it. That's not the same as never meaning it. In London, "no" is considered a little rude, so we hide it inside compliments, tea and invitations to reflect. The skill is finding it.

### 장면1 — 번역 게임

JAMES: Shall we play a game? You tell me what Oliver said. I'll tell you what he meant.

NOAH: Okay. When we suggested three years with automatic renewal, Fiona said, "That's not bad at all."

JAMES: That's a genuine yes. Good. "Not bad at all" means "rather good." Point to you.

SOFIA: See? I was right about something.

ELENA: Next one. Oliver said, "With the greatest respect, Dr. Ruiz."

JAMES: Ooh. That means, "I think you're wrong. Possibly an idiot."

ELENA: I knew it. Zoe's card said so.

JAMES: Who's Zoe?

SOFIA: Our marketing head. She made a card. Ten British phrases. We read it on the plane.

JAMES: Clever woman. Did you read it, Noah?

NOAH: I read it. Then I forgot it. In the room, everything sounded friendly.

JAMES: That's the point. Friendly is the packaging. Not the product.

NOAH: Okay. When Elena explained our red line, Oliver said, "I hear what you say."

JAMES: Oh dear. That means, "I disagree. And I don't want to discuss it further."

SOFIA: Really? I thought it meant he was listening.

JAMES: He was listening. That's why he stopped talking about it.

ELENA: Then Sofia asked, "Can we agree in principle?" He said, "That's quite interesting. Let me reflect."

JAMES: "Quite interesting" is no. "Let me reflect" is no, but later. Together, they're a firm no.

NOAH: And "Close is a lovely word"?

JAMES: That's Oliver being charming. It means, "You think we're close. How sweet."

SOFIA: Oh, no.

ELENA: Last one. I asked him directly. "Is point five a deal breaker?" He said, "A very interesting challenge."

JAMES: Ah. "Very interesting challenge." Translation: "Yes. Absolutely. But I'm too polite to say so."

NOAH: And the email? "Thank you for a most interesting day. Do enjoy London."

JAMES: "Do enjoy London" means, "Your business here is finished. Go and see a museum."

SARAH: The British Museum is free. It's very good for disappointment.

NOAH: I walked out of that office telling everyone we were very close, and when Elena warned me in the taxi, I didn't want to listen. So I spent a whole day hearing yes. And he spent a whole day saying no.

JAMES: Very politely. Over nine kinds of tea.

ELENA: I said it in the taxi. Sofia, I'm not saying "I told you so."

SOFIA: You're saying it with your eyebrows.

ELENA: Only one eyebrow. The other one is being supportive.

NOAH: It's not Sofia's fault. I was in charge, and I was the one telling everyone it was a yes. I heard what I wanted to hear, because I needed this deal to work and I was too tired to notice the signals. That's on me.

JAMES: That's the most British thing you've said all night. Self-criticism. Have another pint.

SARAH: Noah, don't feel bad. Americans say "awesome" about sandwiches. We say "not bad" about weddings.

JAMES: My mother is Nigerian. She says exactly what she means. Loudly. I grew up in Peckham, so at home I heard every word directly, and at school I had to decode every teacher's "interesting." I had to learn both languages.

### 장면2 — 왜 노였는가, 그리고 다음 이메일

ELENA: James, why no? We agreed on price, territory, volumes, everything except point five. And point five isn't even about money for them. It's just a rule about who can buy the product. I don't understand why that alone kills the whole deal.

JAMES: Because point five is his business. How much of his revenue comes from beauticians?

SOFIA: Fiona said sixty percent of the market. She didn't say their revenue.

JAMES: About the same, I'd guess. Maybe more. His reps see beauticians every morning.

NOAH: So our termination clause threatens their main customers.

JAMES: Exactly. One rep. One mistake. One syringe in the wrong car. He loses you.

ELENA: Then he should separate the teams.

JAMES: For six thousand syringes? He'd need new reps, new cars, new managers. That's a whole second company for one product that only sells to forty percent of the market. It doesn't pay, and Oliver can count.

NOAH: So he's not being difficult. He's being rational.

JAMES: He's being rational. And kind. In British, kind is worse. Kind wastes your time.

SOFIA: What should we do? Push harder?

JAMES: No. Pushing makes Oliver politer. And politer means further away.

NOAH: Then what? Wait?

JAMES: Write to him. Tonight. Short. Warm. One clear question. Thank him, show that you understand his position, and then ask him directly whether he can accept your terms. Make it easy for him to say no.

ELENA: Easy to say no? We want a yes.

JAMES: You want the truth. A yes or a no. Not three more weeks of "reflecting."

NOAH: Okay. Help me write it. Sofia, open your laptop.

SOFIA: Ready. "Dear Oliver. Thank you so much for an amazing day. We're super excited—"

JAMES: Stop. Delete "amazing." Delete "super." You sound like a golden retriever.

SARAH: A very nice golden retriever. But still.

JAMES: Try this. "Dear Oliver, thank you for a very useful day. We appreciated your candour."

NOAH: Candour? He wasn't candid at all.

JAMES: Exactly. Now he'll feel slightly guilty. Guilt makes the British honest.

ELENA: That's brilliant. And slightly evil.

JAMES: Next line. "We understand point five may be difficult for your business model."

SOFIA: Got it. Then the question?

JAMES: "Our position on point five will not change. Could you let us know by Friday whether you can accept it?"

NOAH: "Will not change." That's very direct. For British.

JAMES: It's direct about you. Polite about him. That's the trick.

ELENA: Then sign it, Noah. "Kind regards." Not "Cheers." Not "Best!!"

### 절정·전환 — 밤 10시, 문 닫기 전

NOAH: Sent. James, thank you. Can I ask what you do now? Exactly?

JAMES: I advise. Clinics. Training schools. A parliamentary working group on cosmetic procedures.

SARAH: He's being modest. That's also British. He wrote half their report.

ELENA: You trained injectors before. Where?

JAMES: An academy in Manchester. I ran clinical training for six years. Doctors and nurses only.

NOAH: Why did you leave?

JAMES: They started a three-day course. For beauticians. Very profitable. Three days to learn what took me twelve years in A and E, including what to do when a vessel blocks. I told them it wasn't safe. They said it was popular. I resigned the same week.

ELENA: Same week? That's a red line.

JAMES: My mother's influence. She doesn't believe in "reflecting."

NOAH: James, if Oliver says no, what are our options in the UK?

JAMES: Honestly? Most good distributors sell to beauticians. The ones who don't are tiny.

SOFIA: So we're stuck.

JAMES: Or you go direct. Your own team. Your own warehouse. Nurses selling to nurses.

NOAH: Direct. In a country we don't understand. After today, that's clear.

JAMES: Then hire someone who does understand. Not a translator. Someone who knows the clinics, the nurse prescribers, the training schools, and which doors open only if the right person knocks. In this market, trust is local, and it takes years to build.

ELENA: Someone like you.

JAMES: Ha. No. I don't work for companies anymore. Companies start three-day courses.

NOAH: Ours doesn't. Ours lost a hundred thousand dollars refusing to sell to a chain. It hurt, and some people thought we were crazy. But we didn't change the rule then, and we're not changing it for Oliver now.

JAMES: That's quite interesting.

SOFIA: Wait. Is that a British no?

JAMES: …That one I actually meant. Don't tell anyone.

SARAH: Last orders! James, they're closing. And you have clinic at eight.

JAMES: Noah. Here's my card. If Oliver says no, call me. For advice. Only advice.

NOAH: Only advice. Understood.

ELENA: He said "only" twice. James, is that a British yes?

### 마무리 — 다음 날 오전 8시, 호텔 조식당

SOFIA: Noah. Your phone. It's Fiona. Not Oliver. Fiona.

NOAH: Fiona, good morning. Thank you for calling.

FIONA: Noah, I read your email. "Candour." Very clever. Who wrote it?

NOAH: A friend. A British one. Finally.

FIONA: Then I'll be candid. Oliver won't say it. So I will. It's a no. On point five. Too much of our business is with beauticians, and we can't risk all of it on one rep making one mistake. I'd rather tell you today than let you wait for weeks.

NOAH: Thank you. Honestly. That's the most useful thing anyone has said in two days.

FIONA: For what it's worth, I'd have signed. I liked your doctor. She frightened me.

ELENA: Tell her I'll take that as a compliment.

NOAH: So. No distributor. No market knowledge. And a CE mark in ten weeks.

SOFIA: And an American who can't read British emails.

ELENA: Noah, you're smiling. Why are you smiling?

NOAH: Because a clear no is better than a polite maybe. For two days I was waiting for an answer that was never coming. Now we know where we stand, we know what we won't change, and we know who to call. Now we can move.

SOFIA: Move where?

NOAH: Direct. Our own team. And first, a lawyer who knows Europe. Then a call to James.

ELENA: For advice. Only advice.

### ② 비즈니스 컬처 노트 — 영국식 완곡어법(understatement)
영국 비즈니스 영어는 **불일치를 직접 말하지 않고, 칭찬과 거절을 모두 절제해서 표현**한다. "Not bad at all"은 진짜 칭찬이지만, "quite interesting", "I hear what you say", "with the greatest respect"는 대부분 반대나 거절이다. 미국식 영어는 반대로 감정을 **과장(awesome, amazing, super excited)** 하므로, 미국인은 영국식 공손함을 긍정으로 오해하기 쉽다. 대응법은 세 가지다. ① **분위기가 아니라 구체적 질문에 대한 답**을 들어라. ② 후속 이메일은 **짧고 따뜻하게, 분명한 질문 하나와 기한**을 담아라. ③ 상대가 **쉽게 "아니요"라고 할 수 있게** 만들어라. 그래야 시간 낭비 없이 진실을 들을 수 있다.

### ③ 실무 템플릿 — British → Plain English 번역표 & 후속 이메일

> **Subject:** Point five — a clear answer
> Dear Oliver,
> Thank you for a very useful day. We appreciated your candour.
> We understand point five may be difficult for your business model. Our position on point five will not change.

### ④ 긴 발화 분석

> **NOAH (장면1):** "It's not Sofia's fault. I was in charge, and I was the one telling everyone it was a yes. I heard what I wanted to hear, because I needed this deal to work and I was too tired to notice the signals. That's on me."

| 구조 | 영어 | 기능 |
|---|---|---|
| ① 동료 보호 | It's not Sofia's fault. | 책임을 다른 사람에게 돌리지 않겠다고 먼저 선언 |
| ② 책임 인정(사실) | I was in charge, and I was the one telling everyone it was a yes. | 내 역할과 실제 행동을 구체적으로 인정 |
| ③ 원인 분석 | I heard what I wanted to hear, because I needed this deal to work and I was too tired to notice the signals. | 변명이 아니라 실수의 원인을 because로 설명 |
| ④ 마무리(소유) | That's on me. | 짧은 한 문장으로 책임을 확정 |

**활용 패턴:** "It's not X's fault." → 내 역할 인정 → because 원인 → "That's on me."
**연결 표현:** I was in charge(내가 책임자였다) · I was the one -ing(~한 사람은 나였다) · I heard what I wanted to hear(듣고 싶은 걸 들었다) · That's on me(내 책임이다)
**연습:** 팀원의 보고 실수가 사실은 내 판단 착오였던 상황에서, 같은 구조로 40단어 안팎의 사과를 말해 보기.

## 한국어 번역

### 도입 — 밤 8시, 클래펌의 펍

사라: 노아! 여기요. 벽난로 옆. 세 자리 맡아 뒀어요. 싸워서 얻었어요.

노아: 사라, 와 줘서 고마워요. 목요일 밤에. 한 시간 전에 연락했는데.

사라: 문자에 "긴급. 영국 문제."라고 했잖아요. 참을 수가 없었죠. 이쪽은 제임스.

제임스: 제임스 오카포. 사라가 곤경에 빠진 미국인이 있다더군요. 구경하러 왔어요.

엘레나: 엘레나 루이즈. 고마워요. 미국인은 이 사람이에요. 전 멕시코계 미국인. 반만 곤경이에요.

소피아: 소피아 알바레스. 영업. 잘 됐다고 생각한 사람이 저예요.

제임스: 아. 그런 날이군요. 뭐 마실래요?

노아: 추천하는 걸로요. 오늘은 확실히 제가 영국 관련 결정을 하면 안 돼요.

제임스: 그럼 비터 한 파인트. 분위기에 맞네요.

사라: 제임스는 12년간 응급실 간호사였어요. 그래서 무서운 게 없죠. 그다음엔 몇 년 동안 시술자를 교육했고요. 누가 올리버를 통역해 줄 수 있다면 바로 이 사람이에요. 이 업계 사람을 다 알고, 다들 이 사람을 알거든요.

제임스: 다들 서로 알아요. 런던은 지하철 노선도가 있는 마을이죠.

엘레나: 올리버 그랜트 알아요?

제임스: 올리버를 모르는 사람은 없죠. 좋은 사람. 훌륭한 차. 절대 "아니요"라고 안 해요.

소피아: 우리도 그렇게 생각했어요! 아니라고 한 적이 없어요.

제임스: 네. 말을 안 하죠. 그게 그런 뜻이 아니라는 거랑은 같지 않아요. 런던에선 "아니요"가 좀 무례하게 여겨져서, 칭찬이랑 차랑 "생각해 보겠다"는 말 속에 숨겨요. 그걸 찾아내는 게 기술이죠.

### 장면1 — 번역 게임

제임스: 게임 할까요? 올리버가 뭐라고 했는지 말해요. 무슨 뜻이었는지 알려 줄게요.

노아: 좋아요. 자동 갱신 조건으로 3년을 제안했을 때, 피오나가 "전혀 나쁘지 않네요"라고 했어요.

제임스: 그건 진짜 예스예요. 좋아요. "전혀 나쁘지 않다"는 "꽤 좋다"는 뜻이죠. 당신 점수.

소피아: 봐요? 뭔가 하나는 맞혔잖아요.

엘레나: 다음. 올리버가 "최대한의 존경을 담아, 루이즈 박사님"이라고 했어요.

제임스: 오. 그건 "당신이 틀렸다고 생각해요. 어쩌면 바보라고도."라는 뜻이에요.

엘레나: 그럴 줄 알았어요. 조이 카드에도 그렇게 돼 있었어요.

제임스: 조이가 누구예요?

소피아: 우리 마케팅 책임자. 카드를 만들었어요. 영국식 표현 열 개. 비행기에서 읽었죠.

제임스: 똑똑한 분이네요. 노아, 읽었어요?
