## 대본

### 도입 — 오전 8시, 세 도시 화상회의

ANNELIES: Good afternoon. Or good morning, Texas. Annelies de Vries, Brussels. This is Imogen Price, our London office.

IMOGEN: Hello, everyone. I do UK advertising law. Which means I say no a lot. Politely.

NOAH: Please don't be too polite. We had a very polite no last month. It cost us three weeks.

ANNELIES: Don't worry. I'm Dutch. I'll say no very clearly. Imogen will translate it into British.

DIANE: I found them, Noah. They're the best in Europe for medical devices and privacy. Also the most expensive.

ANNELIES: Correct on both. Shall we begin? I read everything you sent. Forty-two documents.

ZOE: Including the campaign deck? I'm Zoe. Marketing. That's my baby.

ANNELIES: Including your baby. Your baby needs some changes. Not a new baby. Changes.

NINA: Nina Park. Product. I built Veyra Circle. Is my baby in trouble too?

ANNELIES: Your baby lives on American servers. In Europe, that's a big conversation. Health data about European patients is protected very strictly here, and every time it crosses the Atlantic, someone like me has to explain why that is legal and safe.

ELENA: Elena Ruiz. Medical. I have no babies in this meeting. I'm here to say "yes, remove it."

IMOGEN: A doctor who likes rules. How refreshing.

NOAH: Here's the thing. Our CE certificate comes in early June. We want to launch in the UK right after, because every month we wait is a month without revenue there. So I need to know today what could stop us.

ANNELIES: Then today we decide what you can say. What you can collect. And where you can keep it.

DIANE: Three questions. I'm writing them down. In writing.

### 장면1 — 무엇을 말할 수 있는가

IMOGEN: Let's start with advertising. Zoe, your slogan. "The filler you can take back."

ZOE: Please don't kill it. Everyone in America knows it.

IMOGEN: I won't kill it. But "take back" is a claim. What exactly can you prove?

ELENA: That it dissolves with hyaluronidase three point one times faster than leading fillers. In our clinical data.

ANNELIES: And is that number in your CE technical documentation? The same number? The same conditions?

ELENA: Yes. Katrin made us check every decimal.

ANNELIES: Good. Then the slogan stays. But every ad must point to that claim. Never stronger.

ZOE: Never stronger. Okay. What about "three times faster"? We round it.

ANNELIES: Rounding down is fine. Three point one becomes three. Rounding up is a lawsuit.

ZOE: Noted. Next slide. Linda's before-and-after photos. Our best-performing ad. Ever.

ANNELIES: In Germany, no. Not to the public.

ZOE: No? Not even with consent? Linda signed everything.

ANNELIES: Consent doesn't matter. German courts treat filler injections like cosmetic surgery, and for surgery, the law protects the public from being tempted by dramatic results. So before-and-after ads to the public are banned, even when the patient is happy to share.

ELENA: Good. I never liked those photos. Remember my fight about them?

ZOE: I remember. I still have the scars. What about the UK?

IMOGEN: In the UK, photos are allowed. With conditions. No retouching. No filters. Real results only.

ZOE: Real results. Fine. We never retouched Linda. She'd kill us.

IMOGEN: Second. No ads aimed at under-eighteens. That's strict. Even indirectly.

ZOE: We'd never target teenagers.

IMOGEN: Not deliberately. But your influencer, Brooke Kendall. What's her UK audience?

ZOE: Let me check. …Thirty-one percent under eighteen. Oh.

IMOGEN: Oh, indeed. Her posts would need age restrictions, so that under-eighteens never see them. And clear labels. "Ad." At the start. Not hidden at the end after twenty hashtags. If she gets it wrong, it's your brand in the headline, not hers.

ZOE: The "Ask if they're Ready" campaign. Is that an ad? It's safety education.

IMOGEN: If it mentions Veyra, it's an ad. If it only teaches safety, it's education.

ZOE: So two versions. One with our name. One without.

IMOGEN: Exactly. The one without our name can go everywhere. Schools, even. The other one, carefully.

ZOE: Third. Spring promotion. "Twenty percent off your first Veyra treatment. This month only."

IMOGEN: No. Time-limited discounts pressure people into deciding quickly, and a patient should choose an injection based on safety and need, not because a price ends on Friday. For a medical procedure, the regulator hates that.

ELENA: So do I. I hated it in America too.

ZOE: Elena, you're enjoying this.

ELENA: A little. I've waited five years for a lawyer to agree with me.

ANNELIES: Last point. Veyra Circle rewards. Patients get points?

NINA: Only for Recover skincare. Never for treatments. Zoe fought for that.

ANNELIES: Good instinct. But in Germany, even that may look like an incentive. Remove points there.

ZOE: So let me summarise. Slogan stays. Photos, UK only. Brooke, age-gated. No discounts. No points in Germany. In America, we could say almost anything that was true. Here, being true is only the beginning. We can't make half our claims here.

### 장면2 — 무엇을 수집하고, 어디에 두는가

ANNELIES: Now, data. Nina, what does Veyra Circle collect?

NINA: Name. Email. Treatment date. Clinic. Product serial number. Optional photos. Optional symptom reports.

ANNELIES: Treatment and symptoms are health data. Under GDPR, that's a special category. Extra protection.

NOAH: What does that mean in practice?

ANNELIES: You need a lawful basis. For health data in a patient app, that's usually explicit consent. Clear. Separate. Recorded. Not hidden in the terms and conditions, not a box that's already ticked. The patient must actively say yes, and be able to say no later.

NINA: We already ask separately. And marketing emails are opt-in. Off by default.

ANNELIES: Off by default? Really?

NINA: Really. Our opt-in users open three times more emails.

ANNELIES: Finally, an American company that did something right. Imogen, write that down. It's historic.

IMOGEN: Noted. With a small celebration.

ANNELIES: Now the harder part. The serial scans. The clinics scan every syringe. Who's in that data?

NINA: The serial number. The clinic. The injector. The date. And in the US, the patient's name.

ANNELIES: Why the patient's name?

NINA: …Honestly? Because the first version had a name field. Nobody removed it.

ELENA: Do we use it? For safety?

NINA: No. For a recall, we trace serial to clinic. The clinic finds the patient, because the clinic already has the medical record. We never needed the name, and keeping data we don't need just means more to protect and more to lose.

ANNELIES: Then remove it. Serial, clinic, date. The clinic keeps the identity. That's called pseudonymisation. Regulators love it.

DIANE: And the lawful basis for scans? We can't ask every patient for consent at the injection.

ANNELIES: You don't need to. Device traceability is a legal obligation under the MDR. That's your basis.

ELENA: So the red line is also the law. I like Europe more every minute.

NOAH: And where do we keep the data? Everything is in Virginia now.

ANNELIES: That's data residency. You can transfer EU data to the US. Legally. With the right agreements. But it's fragile.

IMOGEN: The rules for transfers have been struck down twice in ten years. Courts change their minds.

ANNELIES: My advice? Keep European data in Europe. It costs a little more and takes some work now, but then the next court ruling isn't your problem. You'll sleep better, and so will I, because I won't have to call you at midnight.

NINA: Our cloud provider has a Frankfurt region. A separate EU database. UK data too.

NOAH: How long?

NINA: For the scan system, four weeks. For the full patient app, ten weeks. Maybe twelve. With testing.

ANNELIES: Also, three more things. An EU representative. A data protection officer. And an impact assessment.

DIANE: I'll add them to the list. The list is now eleven pages.

ANNELIES: That's short. My last American client had thirty.

### 절정·전환 — 출시 범위를 정하다

NOAH: Okay. Let's back up a second. CE in early June. Patient app needs twelve weeks. That's September.

ZOE: September? The campaign launches with the product. The app is the campaign. Patients sign up at the clinic, they get reminders, they share their results, and that's how happy patients bring their friends. Without the app, I'm launching a product with no community.

NINA: Then we cut corners. Or we launch without the app.

ANNELIES: Please don't cut corners with health data. European fines are a percentage of global revenue.

ZOE: Then delay the launch. Launch in September. Everything together. Properly.

NOAH: Three more months without UK revenue? The board expects Europe this year, and they heard "June" from me. If we delay everything to September, we lose the summer, and I have to explain why one app held back the whole company.

ELENA: Noah, what do we actually need on day one? Not want. Need.

NOAH: Trained injectors. The product. And the scans. The scans are the red line.

NINA: The scans are four weeks. Ready before June.

ZOE: And the app? The patient community? That's how we won America.

ELENA: Zoe, the scans protect patients. The app protects our campaign. Both matter. One matters first. If a syringe goes missing in July, nobody will ask about our community. They'll ask whether we could trace it. The app can wait. The scans can't.

ZOE: …That's unfair. Because it's true.

NOAH: Decision. UK launch in June. Medical professionals only. Serial scans from day one, on European servers. The patient app in the fourth quarter, built properly, with the EU database and the consent Annelies described. Nobody cuts corners.

ZOE: And my campaign?

NOAH: The version without our name. Safety education. It can start now. Before the product.

IMOGEN: That's actually clever. Education first builds trust. The British respect people who don't sell too early.

ZOE: Fine. Education first. But I want Lily's story in it.

NOAH: Ask Lily. Not me.

ZOE: I learned that lesson in year three. I'll ask her.

DIANE: Noah, one more thing. Who runs the UK? Nobody signs anything without a local manager.

NOAH: Interviews start next week. Three candidates.

ELENA: Is James one of them?

NOAH: No. James agreed to help with the interview questions. Only the questions.

ELENA: "Only." Again.

### ② 비즈니스 컬처 노트 — 유럽 진출과 규제 준수
유럽은 하나의 시장처럼 보이지만 **광고 규제는 나라마다 다르다**. 같은 필러 광고라도 영국에서는 조건부로 허용되는 비포·애프터 사진이 독일에서는 금지될 수 있다. 영국은 **18세 미만 겨냥 금지, 압박성 기간 한정 할인 금지, 인플루언서 광고 표시**를 엄격히 본다. 개인정보는 GDPR이 공통 기준이며, 건강 정보는 **특수 범주**로 분류돼 **적법한 처리 근거**(명시적 동의, 법적 의무 등)가 필요하다. 미국 기업이 흔히 놓치는 것은 **데이터 최소화**와 **데이터 소재지**다. 필요 없는 정보는 처음부터 받지 않고, 유럽 데이터는 유럽에 두는 것이 가장 안정적이다. 규제가 많을 때는 "원하는 것"이 아니라 **"첫날 꼭 필요한 것"**으로 출시 범위를 줄이는 결정이 중요하다.

### ④ 긴 발화 분석

> **ELENA (절정·전환):** "Zoe, the scans protect patients. The app protects our campaign. Both matter. One matters first. If a syringe goes missing in July, nobody will ask about our community. They'll ask whether we could trace it. The app can wait. The scans can't."

| 구조 | 영어 | 기능 |
|---|---|---|
| ① 대비(두 선택지의 목적) | The scans protect patients. The app protects our campaign. | 같은 문장 틀로 두 선택지의 "보호 대상"을 나란히 비교 |
| ② 인정 + 우선순위 | Both matter. One matters first. | 상대의 가치를 인정하면서 순서를 정함 |
| ③ 가정 시나리오 | If a syringe goes missing in July, nobody will ask about our community. They'll ask whether we could trace it. | 최악의 상황을 if로 그려 우선순위의 근거를 보여 줌 |
| ④ 결론(대구) | The app can wait. The scans can't. | 짧은 대구로 결정을 기억하기 쉽게 마무리 |

**활용 패턴:** A는 X를, B는 Y를 지킨다 → 둘 다 중요, 하나가 먼저 → if 최악의 시나리오 → "A can wait. B can't."
**연결 표현:** Both matter(둘 다 중요하다) · One matters first(하나가 먼저다) · If…, nobody will ask about…(~하면 아무도 …를 묻지 않는다) · can wait / can't(기다릴 수 있다/없다)
**연습:** 예산이 부족해 신기능과 보안 패치 중 하나만 먼저 해야 하는 상황에서, 같은 구조로 40단어 안팎으로 팀을 설득해 보기.

엘레나: 여기 저녁 7시면 런던은 새벽 1시예요. 누가 새벽 1시에 면접을 봐요?

노아: …내일 오전 7시였어요. 아마. 확인해 볼게요.

조이: 아마요? 노아, 자기 면접이 몇 시인지 몰랐어요?

노아: 내일 오전 7시예요. 다행이다. 저녁은 지켰어요.

엘레나: 그럼 가요. 지금. 런던이 전화하기 전에.

노아: 클레어가 방금 문자했어요. "7시 그대로야? 아니면 또 고양이랑 먹어야 해?"

조이: "또"? 노아.

노아: 가요. 간다고요. 런던엔 내가 아주 흥미로운 회의 중이라고 해 줘요.
