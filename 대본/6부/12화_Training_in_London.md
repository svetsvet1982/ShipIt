### 장면1 — 오전, 겉도는 교육

TASHA: True! Okay. Module one. Vascular occlusion. Who here has ever seen one?

SARAH: …

TASHA: Anyone? Feel free to jump in!

MARGARET: We've all seen one. Some of us have seen fifty.

TASHA: Wow! Okay, awesome. So the first sign is blanching. The skin turns white. Does that make sense?

MARGARET: Yes. It makes sense. It's made sense for thirty years.

TASHA: Great! Then livedo. A purple, net-like pattern. Does that make sense?

FRASER: Is she going to ask that after every sentence?

SARAH: Alistair. Be nice.

FRASER: I am being nice. In Edinburgh, this is nice.

TASHA: Okay! Let's do a quick role-play! Chloe, you're the injector. Margaret, you're the patient!

MARGARET: I'm the patient. With thirty years of nursing. Lovely.

CHLOE: Um. Margaret, your skin looks… white? Are you okay?

MARGARET: I'm in agony, dear. Do something.

CHLOE: I'd stop injecting. Then hyaluronidase. Then massage. Then call…

TASHA: Awesome! Great job, Chloe! Everybody, give Chloe a big hand!

CHLOE: Thanks. I think.

MARGARET: With respect, Tasha. She did it in the wrong order. And you said "awesome."

TASHA: Oh. Sorry. You're right. I was… encouraging her.

MARGARET: Encourage her by correcting her. That's what she paid for. If she leaves today thinking that order was right, she'll do it that way on a real patient, alone, with nobody there to say 'awesome.' In training, kindness means honesty.

ELENA: Can I take module two now? Early?

TASHA: Yes. Please. Go ahead.

ELENA: Dissolution data. Veyra Fill versus three leading fillers. Same enzyme dose. Same conditions. Here's the curve.

FRASER: What's your n?

ELENA: Two hundred forty in the clinical arm. Lab data in triplicate. The full paper is in your folder. Page six.

FRASER: Confidence interval?

ELENA: Two point seven to three point five. So the honest claim is somewhere around three, which is why we say 'three times' and never more. If a sales rep ever tells you four or five, they're wrong, and I'd like you to tell me.

FRASER: …That's honest. I'll read page six.

MARGARET: Finally. Numbers. Without "awesome."

ELENA: Data has no accent. Questions?

CHLOE: Is it… is it okay to ask a basic question?

ELENA: Basic questions save lives. Ask.

CHLOE: If I'm alone in my clinic and it happens, who do I call first? There's no doctor next door, no colleague down the hall. It's just me and the patient, and I honestly don't know what the first five minutes should look like.

ELENA: That's the best question this morning. Let's take a break. Then we answer it properly.

TASHA: Twenty-minute break! Grab some coffee! Or tea! You guys love tea, right?

### 장면2 — 오전 11시, 휴식 시간 복도

TASHA: James. Be honest. On a scale of one to ten?

JAMES: Honestly? A four. Your content is a nine. Your delivery is American.

TASHA: What does that mean? In plain English?

JAMES: Too loud, too happy, and far too many 'awesomes.' These are experienced professionals, some with decades on the wards, and this morning they felt like children at a birthday party. When people feel talked down to, they go quiet and fold their arms.

TASHA: It works in Texas. Nurses love it. They cheer.

JAMES: British nurses don't cheer. They work twelve-hour shifts in A and E. Then they make tea.

TASHA: And "Does that make sense?" Is that bad too?

JAMES: To Margaret, it sounds like "Are you stupid?" She's been a prescriber longer than you've been a nurse.

TASHA: Oh, no. I said it six times.

JAMES: Eleven. I counted. Quietly.

NOAH: Hey! Sorry I'm late. Red-eye from Austin. How's it going? Should I do a quick welcome speech?

JAMES: No. Not today. Today you listen.

NOAH: Just two minutes. About Lily. About why we started. It always works.

JAMES: Noah, these nurses fix other people's disasters every week, often on their own and at the end of a long shift. They don't need your story, however good it is. What they need today is your respect, and the best way to show it is to listen.

NOAH: …Okay. I'll sit at the back.

ELENA: Noah, when did you land?

NOAH: Seven. I came straight here. I'll sleep tonight.

ELENA: You said that in Paris.

TASHA: James, what do I do? I have ninety minutes to fix this.

JAMES: Four things. One. Lower the energy. Talk like a colleague. Not a cheerleader.

TASHA: Colleague. Not cheerleader. Got it.

JAMES: Two. Stop asking "Does that make sense?" Ask "What would you do?" Then wait. Count to ten.

TASHA: Ten seconds of silence? That's forever.

JAMES: In Britain, ten seconds of silence is thinking. Somebody will speak. Probably Margaret.

TASHA: Silence is thinking. Okay. Three?

JAMES: Let them teach. Margaret has seen fifty occlusions, so use her experience instead of competing with it. Four. British cases, not American numbers. A story from a British clinic means more to this room than any statistic from Texas.

TASHA: British cases. Like Chloe's question? Alone in a clinic. No doctor next door.

ELENA: And tell them about the CPD points. They'll care more about that than any high five.

TASHA: CPD points?

JAMES: Continuing professional development. Nurses need it for their registration. I got today accredited. Seven hours.

### 절정·전환 — 오후 1시, 다시 시작

TASHA: Okay. Before we continue, I owe you an apology. This morning, I treated you like beginners, and you're not. Many of you have more experience than I do, so this afternoon I'm going to talk less, ask more, and actually wait for your answers.

MARGARET: …Thank you. That's appreciated.

TASHA: Also, today counts for seven CPD hours. James arranged it.

SARAH: Seven hours? Now you have my full attention.

TASHA: Here's a case. A British case. Chloe's question. You're alone. Upper lip. The skin blanches. What would you do?

NOAH: Ten seconds. Nobody's answering.

CHLOE: Is she waiting for us?

SARAH: She's counting. I can see her lips moving.

MARGARET: Stop injecting. Aspirate if you can. Hyaluronidase, high dose, immediately. Don't wait for the doctor.

TASHA: Margaret, you've seen more occlusions than I have. Teach me. What's the mistake people make?

MARGARET: They panic about being alone, so they phone someone first, a doctor or a colleague, just to hear another voice. And they lose three minutes on the phone, which is exactly when the tissue is running out of blood. Those minutes matter most.

CHLOE: That's exactly what I'd do. I'd call someone.

MARGARET: Treat first, dear. Phone second. The enzyme doesn't need your phone call.

TASHA: That's our First Ten Seconds card. Margaret just explained it better than I ever have.

FRASER: Can I add something? In Edinburgh, we had a case. The nurse treated first. The patient kept her vision.

TASHA: Tell us. Please. From the beginning.

SARAH: Look at that. Alistair is volunteering. That's never happened.

FRASER: Don't make it a thing, Sarah.

TASHA: Let me put it another way. Everyone here has a story like that. Let's hear three. Then the hands-on session.

JAMES: Noah. Look at the room.

NOAH: They're talking. Arguing. Taking notes.

JAMES: That's a British standing ovation. Without standing. Or ovation.

NOAH: And no fun facts.

TASHA: Hands-on. Pairs, please. Chloe, with Margaret. Correct each other. Honestly.

MARGARET: Come on, dear. I'll be honest. Very.

### ② 비즈니스 컬처 노트 — 청중에 맞춘 교육 진행
미국식 교육은 **높은 에너지, 즉각적 칭찬, 참여형 게임**을 선호하지만, 영국의 숙련된 의료 전문가에게는 이것이 **가볍고 무례하게** 느껴질 수 있다. 특히 "Does that make sense?"를 반복하면 상대의 이해력을 의심하는 것처럼 들린다. 대신 **"What would you do?"**처럼 판단을 묻고, **침묵을 견디며 기다리는 것**이 효과적이다. 영국에서 10초 침묵은 무관심이 아니라 생각하는 시간이다. 숙련된 청중이 있을 때는 **강사가 모든 답을 쥐기보다 그들의 경험을 교재로 삼는 것("Teach me.")**이 신뢰를 만든다. 또한 전문가 교육에서는 **CPD 같은 실질적 혜택**이 어떤 열정보다 강한 동기가 된다.

### ④ 긴 발화 분석

> **TASHA (절정·전환):** "Okay. Before we continue, I owe you an apology. This morning, I treated you like beginners, and you're not. Many of you have more experience than I do, so this afternoon I'm going to talk less, ask more, and actually wait for your answers."

| 구조 | 영어 | 기능 |
|---|---|---|
| ① 예고(사과 선언) | Okay. Before we continue, I owe you an apology. | 본론 전에 사과할 것을 먼저 알림 |
| ② 잘못 인정(구체적) | This morning, I treated you like beginners, and you're not. | 무엇이 잘못이었는지 변명 없이 한 문장으로 |
| ③ 상대 인정 | Many of you have more experience than I do, | 청중의 전문성을 존중하며 관계 회복 |
| ④ 바뀔 행동 약속 | so this afternoon I'm going to talk less, ask more, and actually wait for your answers. | 구체적인 행동 세 가지로 변화를 약속 |

**활용 패턴:** I owe you an apology → 잘못 구체적으로 인정 → 상대 인정 → so I'm going to + 행동 3가지
**연결 표현:** I owe you an apology(사과드려야겠어요) · and you're not(그렇지 않은데도) · so this afternoon I'm going to…(그래서 오후엔 ~하겠다)
**연습:** 회의에서 다른 팀 의견을 계속 끊었던 상황을 가정해, 같은 구조로 40단어 안팎의 사과를 해 보기.

### 장면2 — 오전 11시, 휴식 시간 복도

타샤: 제임스. 솔직하게. 1에서 10으로?

타샤: 아, 이런. 여섯 번 말했어요.

제임스: 열한 번. 세었어요. 조용히.

노아: 안녕! 늦어서 미안해요. 오스틴에서 야간 비행. 어때요? 짧은 환영 연설 할까요?

노아: …알겠어요. 뒤에 앉을게요.

엘레나: 노아, 언제 착륙했어요?

노아: 7시. 바로 왔어요. 오늘 밤에 잘 거예요.

엘레나: 파리에서도 그렇게 말했어요.

### 절정·전환 — 오후 1시, 다시 시작

타샤: 좋아요. 계속하기 전에 사과드릴게요. 오늘 아침 여러분을 초보자처럼 대했는데, 그렇지 않잖아요. 많은 분이 저보다 경험이 많으세요. 그래서 오후엔 덜 말하고, 더 묻고, 여러분의 답을 정말로 기다릴게요.

### 마무리 — 오후 5시, 평가 후
