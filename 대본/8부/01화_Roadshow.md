## 대본

### 도입 — 3월 2일 오전 7시, 뉴욕 호텔 조식당

RUTH: Good morning. Eight days. Three cities. Sixty meetings. And one rule. Same answer. Every time.

NOAH: Every time? Even if I think of a better answer on day five?

RUTH: Especially then. Investors compare notes, and they compare them more than you'd think. If Boston hears a different answer than New York, they don't assume you've improved it; they wonder which one is true, and then they start wondering what else might not be.

PAUL: Ruth ran investor relations at a large device company for twelve years, so she's not guessing about any of this. She's seen three IPOs, and not from the audience. From the inside, where the decisions actually get made.

RUTH: And two that failed. From the inside. Those taught me more.

NOAH: What made them fail?

RUTH: The CEO got bored of his own story. By day four, improvising. By day six, promising things.

PAUL: In a quiet period, promises are illegal. Well. Unwise. Diane says illegal.

RUTH: Three messages. Learn them like your phone number. One. The safety system is the moat.

NOAH: Two. The US and UK are profitable. Asia is an investment. With a timeline.

PAUL: Three. Medical-only isn't a cost. It's the brand. Competitors can't afford to copy it.

RUTH: Good. And when they ask something outside the prospectus?

NOAH: "That's a great question. Everything we can share is in the S-1. Page and section."

RUTH: Perfect. You'll say that forty times. Smile every time. First meeting in twenty-two minutes.

NOAH: Twenty-two minutes. I'll eat something. Claire made me promise. Three meals. Even on a roadshow.

### 장면1 — 3월 2~6일, 미팅들

RUTH: Meeting seven. Day one. A large mutual fund. They'll ask about Asia. Everyone asks about Asia.

SANDRA: Sandra Liu. Healthcare analyst. Why not sell to aestheticians in Asia? It's most of the market.

NOAH: Because medical-only is the brand, and the brand is what doctors are paying for. In Korea and Japan, only doctors may inject anyway, since it's the law there. Selling to aestheticians would cost us the trust that makes the product worth buying.

SANDRA: And in Southeast Asia? Where it isn't the law?

NOAH: Then it's our rule. The strictest rule wins. It's in the S-1. Risk factors, page forty-one.

RUTH: Meeting nineteen. Day two. Same question. Different suit.

PAUL: Halvern. Every meeting asks about Halvern. "What if they copy you?"

NOAH: Then they'd have to stop selling to beauticians. In the UK alone, that's sixty percent of their volume.

RUTH: Good. Same words as Monday. I'm counting.

PAUL: Meeting twenty-six. "Your CTO just changed roles. Is Dr. Raman leaving?"

NOAH: No. She's the Chief Scientist. She chose it. Sixty percent of her time on science. Measured quarterly.

RUTH: You added "measured quarterly." That's new.

NOAH: It's true. It's in the S-1. Management section.

RUTH: Then it stays. Everywhere. From now on.

MARCY: Marcy Feldman. Commonwealth. Boston. Day three. I've read your S-1 twice. Can I ask about the registry?

NOAH: Please.

MARCY: Thirteen hundred vascular events. Every one recorded. Public. Why would a company publish its own complications?

NOAH: Because hiding them is exactly how the last generation of filler companies lost doctors' trust, and once a doctor stops trusting your data, they stop trusting your product. So we publish everything. Ours even includes the recall, which wasn't comfortable to put in.

MARCY: That's the first question I haven't heard on this roadshow. And the first answer I believed.

PAUL: She's a long-only investor. Ten-year horizon. That's the shareholder we want.

RUTH: Day four. Boston. Afternoon. Noah, your voice.

NOAH: …It's going. Twenty-nine meetings. Thirty-one to go.

RUTH: Honey and ginger. Then let Paul open the next three. You do Q and A only.

PAUL: "Veyra Aesthetics makes a filler that lasts and dissolves three times faster. Our safety system is our moat."

NOAH: …

RUTH: What is it?

NOAH: I'm hearing it from outside for the first time. It sounds like a brochure.

RUTH: It is a brochure. A very compliant one.

NOAH: Ruth, on day one, I believed every word. Now I'm reciting it. Like a hostage video.

RUTH: That's the day-four problem. The two CEOs who failed reached it too.

NOAH: And what should they have done?

RUTH: Changed the story without changing the facts. The numbers stay exactly the same, and so do the three messages, but the way in, the first minute, can be new. Most people can't tell the difference, and that's why they either bore investors or start improvising.

NOAH: Can I open with Seoul? The thirteen minutes? It's public. In the registry. A fact.

RUTH: No patient names. No promises. No "this will never happen again." Then yes.

NOAH: Then from tomorrow, I start in Seoul. At ten fourteen p.m.

NOAH: "At ten fourteen p.m. in Seoul, a doctor saw a patient's skin turn white. By ten twenty-seven, it was pink again."

JONATHAN: And the hedge funds get less. They'll complain.

### ② 비즈니스 컬처 노트 — 로드쇼와 공모가
IPO 로드쇼에서 경영진은 며칠 동안 수십 번 같은 질문을 받는다. 핵심은 **메시지의 일관성**이다. 투자자들은 서로 정보를 비교하기 때문에, 도시마다 답이 달라지면 신뢰가 무너진다. 동시에 같은 말을 반복하다 보면 피치가 생기를 잃는다. 해법은 **사실은 그대로 두고 이야기(오프닝, 사례)를 새로 고치는 것**이다. 조용한 기간에는 투자 설명서(S-1)에 없는 예측이나 약속을 해서는 안 된다. 수요가 많을 때 주관사는 공모가를 최대한 올리려 하지만, 경영진은 **가격과 함께 주주의 질**을 고려해야 한다. 조금 낮은 가격으로 **장기 투자자에게 우선 배정**하는 것은 "테이블에 돈을 남기는" 대신 10년의 안정성을 사는 선택이다.

### ④ 긴 발화 분석

> **RUTH (도입):** "Especially then. Investors compare notes, and they compare them more than you'd think. If Boston hears a different answer than New York, they don't assume you've improved it; they wonder which one is true, and then they start wondering what else might not be."

| 구조 | 영어 | 기능 |
|---|---|---|
| ① 결론(짧게 반박) | Especially then. | 상대의 예외 제안을 두 단어로 뒤집기 |
| ② 전제(사실) | Investors compare notes, and they compare them more than you'd think. | 판단의 근거가 되는 시장의 현실 제시 |
| ③ 가정(if…) | If Boston hears a different answer than New York, they don't assume you've improved it; | 구체적 상황을 가정해 상대 관점을 바로잡기 |
| ④ 파급(연쇄 결과) | they wonder which one is true, and then they start wondering what else might not be. | 작은 불일치가 전체 신뢰로 번지는 과정을 단계적으로 |

**활용 패턴:** 짧은 결론 → 현실(전제) → If 가정 → "and then…" 연쇄 결과
**연결 표현:** more than you'd think(생각보다 훨씬) · they don't assume…; they wonder…(~라고 여기지 않고 ~를 의심한다) · and then they start…(그러다 ~하기 시작한다)
**연습:** 고객마다 다른 할인율을 주자는 동료에게 같은 구조로 40단어 안팎으로 반대해 보기.

## 한국어 번역

### 도입 — 3월 2일 오전 7시, 뉴욕 호텔 조식당

루스: 좋은 아침이에요. 8일. 3개 도시. 미팅 60회. 그리고 규칙 하나. 같은 답. 매번.

노아: 매번요? 5일 차에 더 좋은 답이 생각나도요?

루스: 특히 그때요. 투자자들은 서로 메모를 비교하는데, 생각보다 훨씬 많이 해요. 보스턴이 뉴욕과 다른 답을 들으면, 답이 나아졌다고 생각하지 않아요. 어느 쪽이 사실인지 의심하고, 그다음엔 또 뭐가 사실이 아닐지 의심하기 시작하죠.

폴: 루스는 대형 의료기기 회사에서 12년간 IR을 했어요. 그러니 이 중 어느 것도 짐작으로 하는 말이 아니에요. IPO를 세 번 봤는데, 객석에서가 아니에요. 안에서요. 실제로 결정이 내려지는 곳에서.

루스: 그리고 실패한 두 번도요. 안에서. 그게 더 많이 가르쳐 줬어요.

노아: 왜 실패했어요?

루스: CEO가 자기 이야기에 질렸어요. 4일 차엔 즉흥으로. 6일 차엔 약속을 하고 있었죠.

폴: 조용한 기간에 약속은 불법이에요. 음. 현명하지 않죠. 다이앤은 불법이래요.

루스: 메시지 세 개. 전화번호처럼 외워요. 하나. 안전 시스템이 해자다.

노아: 둘. 미국과 영국은 흑자. 아시아는 투자. 일정이 있는.

폴: 셋. 의료인 전용은 비용이 아니다. 브랜드다. 경쟁사는 따라 할 여유가 없다.

루스: 좋아요. 그럼 투자 설명서 밖의 걸 물으면요?
